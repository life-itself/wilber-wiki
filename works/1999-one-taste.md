# One Taste

*The Journals of Ken Wilber*

**Year:** 1999 (rev. 2000) · **Format:** Book

## Description

A year of Wilber's personal journals — daily reflection, meditation notes, and
commentary on contemporaries — giving a more informal, first-person view of his life
and practice than his theoretical books.

## In-Depth Overview

*One Taste* is structured as a year of dated journal entries (drawn from around 1997),
mixing three things in no particular order from one entry to the next: reports from
Wilber's own daily meditation practice, informal restatements of his theoretical
positions (pitched much more casually than in the systematic books), and commentary —
sometimes warm, sometimes combative — on contemporary teachers, critics, and public
figures he crosses paths with. The title refers to the nondual state of consciousness
in which the ordinary distinction between witnessing awareness and everything witnessed
collapses — "one taste" — and the book is less an argument for that state than an
attempt to show, entry by entry, what a working contemplative life oriented around it
actually looks like day to day.

It's the most personal and least systematic of the "theory" books, closer in spirit to
*Grace and Grit* than to *SES*, and gives a clearer sense of Wilber's personality — wit,
combativeness with critics, self-deprecation, genuine practice discipline — than
anything in the more formal work. Useful as a counterweight to the density of *SES* and
*Integral Psychology*: the same underlying claims, but seen from the inside of a
practicing life rather than laid out as a system.
