# The evolution of Wilber's thought

Wilber I, II, III, IV, and V name successive formulations of Ken Wilber's thinking.
They help explain why two books by the same author can offer different accounts of
development or spirituality. They are not stages a reader must pass through, and
they do not make every later book a theoretical advance over every earlier one.

For a practical selection, see [What to read, and why](reading-guide.md).
For all titles and dates, use the [chronological catalog](catalog.md).

## The five phases at a glance

The dates below locate the main publications, rather than claim exact start and end
dates for Wilber's thinking. Wilber's own summary in *Integral Psychology*, chapter 9,
note 15, identifies phases I–IV and explicitly calls that book a phase-IV work.
The fifth phase is the later post-metaphysical development, discussed in
[Ali Akalin's publisher guide](https://www.shambhala.com/integral-theory-primer-a-readers-guide-to-ken-wilber-by-ali-akalin/).

| Phase | What changes | Representative books |
|---|---|---|
| **I — The early spectrum** · late 1970s | A spectrum connects psychological and spiritual accounts of consciousness; the early formulation includes a return to lost wholeness. | [The Spectrum of Consciousness](1977-spectrum-of-consciousness.md) (1977); [No Boundary](1979-no-boundary.md) (1979) |
| **II — Development** · around 1980–82 | Spiritual development is understood as growth beyond ordinary ego development, rather than recovery of an infantile condition. | [The Atman Project](1980-the-atman-project.md) (1980); [Up from Eden](1981-up-from-eden.md) (1981) |
| **III — Levels and lines** · 1980s | Different capacities can develop unevenly; a person cannot be adequately described by one position on a single ladder. | [A Sociable God](1983-a-sociable-god.md) (1983, near the II/III transition); [Eye to Eye](1984-eye-to-eye.md) (1983); [Transformations of Consciousness](1986-transformations-of-consciousness.md) (1986) |
| **IV — The four-quadrant synthesis** · mid-1990s–2000 | Individual development is placed within interacting subjective, behavioral, cultural, and social dimensions. | [Sex, Ecology, Spirituality](1995-sex-ecology-spirituality.md) (1995); [A Brief History of Everything](1996-a-brief-history-of-everything.md) (1996); [Integral Psychology](2000-integral-psychology.md) (2000) |
| **V — Post-metaphysics and perspectives** · early 2000s onward | Greater emphasis on how perspectives and methods disclose their objects, and how spiritual states relate to developmental structures. | Kosmos II excerpts; [Integral Spirituality](2006-integral-spirituality.md) (2006). [The Religion of Tomorrow](2017-the-religion-of-tomorrow.md) is a later extended treatment. |

## What the changes mean for reading

**I → II: a substantive revision.** The early unity model and the later developmental
model do not tell quite the same story. Wilber's retrospective account distinguishes
recovering lost spiritual potential from developing capacities that were not already
fully present. Read *The Atman Project* after *Spectrum* when you want to understand
that turn. *Up from Eden* extends the developmental project into cultural history;
it is not simply the same book with different examples.

**II → III: development becomes more differentiated.** Someone can be sophisticated
in one capacity and immature in another. Levels and relatively independent lines
give Wilber a way to describe that unevenness. *Transformations of Consciousness*
also brings the discussion into dialogue with clinical psychology and contemplative
practice. It is a collaborative volume, not a solo statement of everything Wilber thinks.

**III → IV: the context becomes part of the model.** The four quadrants broaden the
account beyond a person's interior development. *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* gives
this synthesis its expansive treatment; *Integral Psychology* works through the
psychological detail. Reading both is useful because their emphases differ, even
though the second belongs to the same broad phase.

These three transitions follow Wilber's own account in *Integral Psychology*,
chapter 9, note 15. They describe his theory's claims, not an independent validation
of its developmental model.

**IV → V: how do we know, and from which perspective?** Post-metaphysics questions
the treatment of spiritual maps as straightforward descriptions of a ready-made
cosmic order. Integral methodological pluralism gives methods and perspectives a
more explicit place. *Integral Spirituality* is a useful book-length entry to this
development. Its state–stage distinction also helps explain why contemplative
attainment and psychological maturity need not advance together.

This last distinction has a prehistory: in *Integral Psychology*, chapter 12,
note 12, Wilber already discusses states interpreted through different structures
and points back to *A Sociable God*. It would be misleading to suggest he first
noticed states and stages in 2006. For a reader's account of the later formulation,
see [Rufus's notes on Integral Spirituality](../notes/2019-integral-spirituality-notes.md).

## Why the boundaries are approximate

Books take time to write; essay collections bring together pieces from different
years; later editions can add a newer framing to an older work. Even Wilber's
retrospective labels are not perfectly mechanical: *Integral Psychology* refers
back to a phase-II formulation in 1983, while *A Sociable God* also helps explain
the movement toward phase III.

[Paul Helfrich's overview](https://www.integralworld.net/helfrich.html) dates phases
using composition and conceptual development, whereas the shorter
[Integral World overview](https://www.integralworld.net/phases.html) emphasizes
publication periods. Their dates differ. The conceptual transitions matter more
than assigning every book to an exact interval.

Memoirs and anthologies need another kind of description. *Grace and Grit* is worth
reading for lived experience; its 1991 publication does not make it the next
systematic revision after *Transformations*. A 2008 anthology can contain much
earlier thinking. A retitled edition does not create a new phase.

## What happened to the Kosmos trilogy?

**Sex, Ecology, Spirituality is volume I.** The project did not become a completed
three-volume sequence in the publisher and bibliography records checked for this
guide (September 2026). The later material needs to be distinguished from the
publication of finished, numbered volumes.

In the revised-edition discussion headed “The Kosmos Trilogy,” Wilber says that
volume II was substantially written and volume III outlined, but gives no firm
release date. Elsewhere in that discussion he uses the tentative volume-II title
*Sex, God, and Gender: The Ecology of Men and Women*. These statements describe the
project at that time, not the current condition of the manuscripts.

Later, excerpts associated with *Kosmic Karma and Creativity* circulated as material
from volume II. A [2008 review](https://integralleadershipreview.com/5098-coda-wilbers-kosmos-2-excerpts/)
lists the introduction and excerpts A, B, C, D, and G. Their subjects include
integrative methods, intersubjectivity, structuralism, and subtle energies.
[Helfrich's overview](https://www.integralworld.net/helfrich.html) connects this
material with the emerging fifth phase. This is evidence of a developing project
and released excerpts, not evidence of a finished volume II.

**Where to read the continuation:** start with *Integral Spirituality* for the later
book-length treatment of methods, perspectives, and spirituality. For the more
technical perspective notation, look for the Kosmos II excerpts; an
[Integral Life discussion](https://integrallife.com/sex-pronouns-and-prepositions/)
connects Excerpt C with the later book. *The Religion of Tomorrow* extends the
spirituality project, but should not be relabeled “volume III.” Neither later book
is presented here as an exact replacement for an unpublished volume.

There is a separate, documented lineage: *The Fourth Turning* was republished as
*Integral Buddhism*, and Wilber calls it a preliminary version of *The Religion of
Tomorrow*. That relationship comes from his
[published introduction](https://www.shambhala.com/future-religion-excerpt-ken-wilber/);
it does not establish completion of the Kosmos trilogy.
