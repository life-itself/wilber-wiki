# Integral Psychology

*Consciousness, Spirit, Psychology, Therapy*

**Year:** 2000 · **Format:** Book

## Description

Systematic attempt to cross-index dozens of Western developmental-psychology models
(Piaget, Kohlberg, Loevinger, Graves, and others) against contemplative stage models
within the AQAL framework; includes Wilber's widely reproduced comparative charts.

## In-Depth Overview

*Integral Psychology* is the most reference-like of Wilber's core books: it
cross-indexes over a hundred developmental models — Western psychological (Piaget,
Kohlberg, Loevinger, Erikson, Gilligan, Kegan, Graves), depth-psychological (Neumann),
cultural-historical (Gebser), and contemplative (Vedantic koshas, Kabbalah, Aurobindo,
Teresa of Ávila, and others) — against a single underlying set of developmental "waves."
The organizing distinction is between developmental **lines** or **streams**
(cognitive, moral, affective, aesthetic, interpersonal, spiritual, and so on, each of
which can develop somewhat independently of the others — someone can be cognitively
very advanced and morally undeveloped, for instance) and the **waves** or levels those
lines pass through. Layered on top of lines and waves are **states** of consciousness
(temporary — anyone can have a peak experience regardless of developmental level),
**quadrants** (the same four-perspective distinction from *SES*, here applied
specifically to psychology), and horizontal **types** (discrete personality or style
variables, such as Myers-Briggs types or gender, that cut across all of the above rather
than ranking on it). A **self system** navigates all of these simultaneously, identifying
with, defending, and integrating material at whatever levels and along whatever lines it
is currently working through.

The bulk of the book's length is in its appendices — the widely reproduced comparative
charts lining up dozens of named developmental models side by side against Wilber's own
scale. It's written more for psychology and clinical/therapy professionals than for a
general audience, functioning as the technical reference volume behind the more
narrative treatments in *SES* and *A Brief History of Everything* — useful to consult
when you want to know exactly where a specific psychologist's model (say, Kohlberg's
moral stages, or Loevinger's ego development) is claimed to map onto Wilber's own
framework, rather than to read start to finish.
