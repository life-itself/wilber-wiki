# Integral Spirituality

*A Startling New Role for Religion in the Modern and Postmodern World*

**Year:** 2006 · **Format:** Book

## Description

Marks Wilber's turn to **"integral post-metaphysics"** — reinterpreting contemplative
states and stages without pre-modern metaphysical assumptions — and introduces the
**Wilber–Combs Lattice** mapping states of consciousness against structures/stages of
development.

## In-Depth Overview

*Integral Spirituality* is Wilber's most technical, meta-theoretical book and the one
where his framework visibly turns a corner. Its central move is **integral
post-metaphysics**: earlier Wilber (and most of the contemplative traditions he draws
on) speak as if there are pre-existing "levels" of reality or structures of the psyche
simply waiting to be discovered through practice — a broadly Platonic, "Great Chain of
Being" picture. Here Wilber argues that's no longer philosophically defensible after
Kant and postmodernism: there is no view from nowhere, no pre-given reality separate
from the perspective enacting it. Developmental structures and stages, on this account,
are *enacted* through evolution rather than *discovered* as already-existing furniture
of the universe — a significant reframing of claims that looked more metaphysically
realist in *SES*.

The book's most widely used practical contribution is the **Wilber–Combs Lattice**
(developed with Allan Combs), which cleanly separates **states** of consciousness from
**stages** of development: states (gross, subtle, causal, witnessing, nondual) are, in
principle, available to a person at *any* developmental stage — anyone can have a
profound nondual peak experience — but how that experience gets interpreted and
integrated afterward depends entirely on the stage the person occupies when they have
it. This directly explains, in Wilber's account, why genuinely gifted meditators can
still hold what look like developmentally immature ethical or political views: state
attainment and stage development are separate axes, not one continuum. The book also
introduces **Integral Methodological Pluralism** — a further subdivision of each of the
four quadrants into inside and outside views (eight "zones" total, e.g. phenomenology
vs. structuralism as the inside/outside methodologies proper to the interior-individual
quadrant), an attempt to give each of the many competing schools of psychology,
sociology, and science a legitimate, non-overlapping methodological home within AQAL
rather than treating them as rivals.

This is denser and more meta-theoretical than *SES* or *A Brief History of Everything*,
and a number of longtime readers have found it a harder read than the more narrative
earlier work — it's really a book about *how to build a theory of everything
responsibly* as much as it is a restatement of the theory itself.
