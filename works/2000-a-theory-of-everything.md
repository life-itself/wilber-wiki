# A Theory of Everything

*An Integral Vision for Business, Politics, Science and Spirituality*

**Year:** 2000 · **Format:** Book

## Description

Short, applied restatement of AQAL aimed explicitly at readers in business, politics,
medicine, and education rather than psychology or spirituality specifically.

## In-Depth Overview

Much shorter than *SES* or *Integral Psychology*, *A Theory of Everything* is the book
where Wilber deliberately steps outside psychology and spirituality and pitches AQAL as
a general-purpose framework — an "operating system" — for readers in business,
politics, medicine, and education who have no particular interest in contemplative
practice. It leans heavily on developmental-**stage** models of value systems (in the
style of Clare Graves' work and Spiral Dynamics, though Wilber presents his own version
rather than Graves' original terms) to explain why people and institutions at different
developmental stages talk past each other on political and organizational questions —
e.g., a traditional/mythic-stage worldview, a modern/rational-achievement worldview, and
a postmodern/pluralistic worldview genuinely can't hear each other's concerns as valid,
in his account, because each is operating from a different center of gravity, not
just a different opinion.

This is the book most directly responsible for AQAL's uptake outside psychology and
spirituality — in conscious-business, leadership, and organizational-development
circles specifically — and functions as a direct precursor to both Integral Institute
(founded 1998, shortly before this book) and the more applied, practice-oriented
*Integral Life Practice* that followed it in 2008.
