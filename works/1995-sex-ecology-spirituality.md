# Sex, Ecology, Spirituality

*The Spirit of Evolution*

**Year:** 1995 (rev. 2001) · **Format:** Book

## Description

**SES.** Volume one of the projected *Kosmos Trilogy* and Wilber's central theoretical
work: the full statement of the four-quadrant **AQAL** model, holons and holarchies, and
an argument that evolution — cosmic, biological, and cultural — has a directionality
toward greater depth and consciousness. Dense and academic; the book this wiki's
annotation work is meant to start with.

## In-Depth Overview

*SES* is the book where Wilber's mature framework gets its full, technical statement —
roughly 800 pages of main text plus extensive endnotes that carry independent arguments
of their own. Part One, "The Patterns of Existence," opens with "The Web of Life" and
then "The Pattern That Connects," where Wilber draws on Arthur Koestler's concept of the
**holon** (a whole that is simultaneously a part of some larger whole — an atom within a
molecule, a cell within an organ, a person within a family) and on Ludwig von
Bertalanffy's general systems theory to lay out roughly twenty tenets that, he argues,
govern how holons behave at every level of reality: self-preservation and
self-adaptation, self-transcendence (the capacity to give rise to genuinely novel,
higher-order wholes) and self-dissolution, and a tendency for holons to organize into
**holarchies** — hierarchies in the strict sense of each level transcending but
including the one below it, not dominating it. Chapter three, "Individual and Social,"
adds a second axis (individual/collective) to the existing interior/exterior axis
already implicit in the holon concept, producing the **four quadrants** that became
Wilber's signature diagram: intentional (I), behavioral (it), cultural (we), and social
(its) — four irreducible perspectives from which any holon, from an atom to a nation,
can be described.

The rest of the book traces this pattern through three domains in sequence: the
evolution of matter into life (cosmology, physics, the emergence of biological
organization), the evolution of life into mind (biological and psychological
development), and the evolution of human consciousness through successive cultural
structures (archaic, magic, mythic, rational, and — prospectively — integral or
"vision-logic," drawing on Jean Gebser). Running underneath all three is Wilber's most
contested claim: that evolution, at every one of these levels, has a real
directionality toward greater depth, complexity, and consciousness — not mere random
drift or exclusively externally-selected change. The second half of the book turns
polemical, arguing that modern science and much of Western philosophy since the
Enlightenment collapsed reality into what Wilber calls **"flatland"** — a picture with
only exteriors (matter, systems, behavior) and no legitimate interiors (meaning,
value, consciousness) — and that postmodernism, while right to critique the arrogance
of a single "view from nowhere," undermines itself when it denies any developmental
hierarchy at all. A revised edition in 2000/2001 added substantial new material
responding to critics of the first edition.

This is the book *SES* — Wilber's central theoretical work and, by his own account, the
first installment of a planned *Kosmos Trilogy* that was never completed as originally
conceived (later books like *The Eye of Spirit* function as companion volumes rather
than numbered sequels). It's also the book that has drawn the most sustained academic
criticism of Wilber's scholarship — most extensively in Jeff Meyerhoff's book-length
critique *Bald Ambition* — largely over how reliably its enormous range of cited source
material is actually represented. Both the ambition and that criticism are worth
tracking here as this wiki's annotation work on the book develops.
