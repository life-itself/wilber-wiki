# The Religion of Tomorrow

*A Vision for the Future of the Great Traditions*

**Year:** 2017 · **Format:** Book

## Description

Wilber's longest and most systematic later work: a proposal for how the world's major
religious traditions could integrate developmental psychology and post-metaphysical
spirituality without losing their contemplative core.

## In-Depth Overview

At roughly 800 pages, *The Religion of Tomorrow* is Wilber's longest and most
systematic later-period book, and functions as an integral-post-metaphysics-era
counterpart to *SES* — a full restatement of the mature system focused specifically on
religion. It's organized in four parts. Part One frames the project as a **"Fourth
Turning"** of the Dharma (extending the traditional three turnings of Buddhist
teaching): a proposal that the world's contemplative traditions can absorb modern and
postmodern developmental psychology without losing their depth. Part Two lays out
**states and structures** of consciousness as two distinct developmental streams —
states (gross, subtle, causal, witnessing, nondual) as temporary and available to
anyone regardless of stage, structures as earned, sequential waves of development one
grows through — the same states/stages distinction from *Integral Spirituality*,
elaborated here at much greater length and applied specifically to spiritual practice
and the interpretation of mystical experience.

Part Three turns clinical: a treatment of **shadow** material and how unintegrated
psychological "baggage" — disowned emotions, unresolved trauma, unconscious defenses —
distorts spiritual practice and experience, drawing on the same 3-2-1 shadow work
introduced practically in *Integral Life Practice*. Part Four assembles the pieces into
a full picture of what an integral spirituality looks like when growing up (structures),
waking up (states), cleaning up (shadow), and — a fourth term used elsewhere in his
later work — showing up (quadrants/practice) are pursued together rather than any one in
isolation. It's the most complete single statement of Wilber's mature system as applied
to religion and contemplative practice specifically, at the cost of being considerably
longer and more repetitive than the shorter core books.

## Relationship to earlier work

In his [published introduction](https://www.shambhala.com/future-religion-excerpt-ken-wilber/),
Wilber describes [The Fourth Turning](2014-the-fourth-turning.md) as a preliminary
version of this book. That shorter work was also republished as
[Integral Buddhism](2018-integral-buddhism.md). This is a documented development
relationship, not evidence that *The Religion of Tomorrow* is a numbered volume
of the [Kosmos trilogy](phases.md#what-happened-to-the-kosmos-trilogy).
