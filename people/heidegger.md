# Martin Heidegger

## Wilber's View

Heidegger shows up in *SES* wearing several different hats, almost all of them
approving. He's cited as a co-founder, with Dilthey and Weber, of the hermeneutic
tradition Wilber leans on for interpreting culture "from within" — the idea that
meaning is never simply read off a text or a ritual but always excavated against a
background of unstated, pre-articulate understanding, "contexts within contexts within
contexts." He's credited as a genuine "pioneer" of poststructuralism, in the specific
sense that his *Destruktion* of rationalist ontology is the philosophical ancestor of
what [[derrida|Derrida]] would later call deconstruction. And in one of the book's more
personal passages, Wilber invokes Heidegger directly on mortality: dread, as Heidegger
said, is the authentic response of the centauric (existential, [[vision-logic]]-capable)
being — not fear of this or that, but the totality of one's being-in-the-world becoming
present to itself as finite. Wilber also singles out Heidegger's notion of
being-in-the-world as one of the postmodern era's real attempts at *integration* — an
effort, alongside [[gebser|Gebser]]'s integral-aperspectivism and
[[habermas|Habermas]]'s communicative action, to reweave what modernity had
differentiated. The one place Wilber pulls back from unqualified endorsement is when
Heidegger gets folded into the general list of Enlightenment critics (with
[[hegel|Hegel]], [[nietzsche|Nietzsche]], Foucault, Derrida) whose assault on modernity's
"repressive side," in Wilber's view, too often ignores modernity's genuine
accomplishments — the same "good news, bad news" charge he levels at the whole
postmodern lineage.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> [!quote]- "Dread, as Heidegger said, is the authentic response of the existential (centauric) being"
> As vision-logic adds up all the possibilities given to the mind's eye, it eventually
> reaches a dismal conclusion: personal life is a brief spark in the cosmic void... dread,
> as Heidegger said, is the authentic response of the existential (centauric) being, a
> dread that calls us back from self-forgetting to self-presence, a dread that seizes
> not this or that part of me (body or persona or ego or mind), but rather the totality
> of my being-in-the-world.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> [!quote]- "The critique by Heidegger, Gebser" — the pre-articulate worldview beneath language
> Linguistic structures aren't really *that* autonomous, because they exist only in the
> context of pre-articulate worldviews that use language without language ever
> registering that fact (the critique by Heidegger, Gebser).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 2, "The Pattern That Connects"

> [!quote]- "The last-named is also a 'pioneer' of poststructuralism"
> He particularly focuses on Bergson, Husserl, and Heidegger, although he had some sharp
> criticisms of them as well (the last-named is also a "pioneer" of poststructuralism,
> and his notion of the *Destruktion* of rationalist ontology appears in Derrida as
> deconstruction).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 5, "The Emergence of Human Nature"

> [!quote]- "Heidegger's centauric being-in-the-world (also an attempt to reweave the fragments)"
> This is the postmodern *integrative vision* we have mentioned often before, in
> connection with Gebser ("integral-aperspectival"), Habermas (whose theory of
> communicative action is specifically designed to integrate the Big Three), and
> Heidegger's centauric being-in-the-world (also an attempt to reweave the fragments).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 11, "Brave New World"
