# Max Weber

## Wilber's View

Weber supplies Wilber with two specific analytic tools rather than a broad worldview to
argue with. First, a typology of rationality: Wilber borrows Weber's distinction between
purposive-rationality (scientific-technological knowledge), formal rationality
(mathematics), and intersubjective or practical rationality (morality and
communication) — a three-way split that anticipates Wilber's own insistence that
"rational" is not one thing but tracks onto the it, we, and I domains of the
[[big-three|Big Three]]. Second, and more load-bearing, Wilber (via [[habermas|Habermas]]'s
quotation of Weber) adopts Weber's account of modernity's "dignity" as the
*differentiation of the value spheres* — science, morality, and art each pursuing
"validity" on its own terms (truth, justice, taste) rather than being fused under one
authority, usually religion. That formulation becomes one of the central structural
claims of the second half of *SES*: the differentiation Weber identifies is real
progress, but when the three spheres go on to *dissociate* from each other entirely
rather than staying integrated, the result is [[flatland|flatland]] — the reduction of
we and I to it. Weber is also named, briefly, as one link in the older Dilthey/Weber/
Heidegger hermeneutic lineage Wilber draws on for reading meaning "from within" a
culture's practices, and as one of the "doctors of modernity" (with Hegel, Marx,
Schiller, Freud, Heidegger) who tried to heal the fragmentation modernity produced.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> [!quote]- "Weber... differentiated between purposive-rationality... formal rationality... and intersubjective or practical rationality"
> The word *rational* is an impossible label; it means a million things to a million
> people... Weber, for example, differentiated between purposive-rationality (such as
> scientific-technological knowledge), formal rationality (such as mathematics), and
> intersubjective or practical rationality (as displayed in morality and communication).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 5, "The Emergence of Human Nature"

> [!quote]- "'Cultural modernity's specific dignity is constituted by what Max Weber called the differentiation of the value spheres...'"
> We have seen that one of the great defining marks of modernity—what has been called
> its genuine *dignity*—was the clear differentiation of the Big Three, the
> differentiation of science (it), morals (we), and art (I)... "Cultural modernity's
> specific dignity is constituted by what Max Weber called the differentiation of the
> value spheres in accord with their own logics."
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 12, "The Collapse of the Kosmos"

> [!quote]- "That is a short summary of the hermeneutic program from Wilhelm Dilthey and Weber and Heidegger"
> To understand and articulate this in a sociological fashion... I must look into the
> whole network of shared social practices and the "background unconscious" of
> linguistically... structured meanings... (That is a short summary of the hermeneutic
> program from Wilhelm Dilthey and Weber and Heidegger down to today with Paul Ricoeur
> and Hans Gadamer and Geertz and Taylor.)
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 4, "A View from Within"
