# Robert Kegan

## Wilber's View

Kegan gets the warmest, least qualified praise of any researcher in *Integral
Psychology* — Wilber calls him "everybody's favorite developmentalist" and includes
himself in that "everybody" without reservation, a tone he doesn't extend even to
Baldwin or Habermas (both of whom get real critiques alongside the praise). What
Wilber credits Kegan with specifically is conceptual clarity about the *mechanism* of
self-development, not just another stage sequence: Kegan's embedding/de-embedding
language — a given wave starts out as the medium you're embedded in and unable to see,
then becomes an object you can look at once you've grown past it — is effectively
Wilber's own "subject of one stage becomes object of the next" formulation (see
[The Proximate Self and the Distal Self](../concepts/proximate-self-and-distal-self.md)),
and Kegan is cited by name, alongside Sri Ramana Maharshi, as one of the researchers
who established that point.

## In *Integral Psychology*

> Robert Kegan...seems to be everybody's favorite developmentalist (count me in). He
> discusses a broad range of developmental issues with insight, exactitude,
> sensitivity, and care.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 7

> Kegan's approach is especially important, in my view, because he so clearly
> elucidates the nature of embedding (identifying) and de-embedding (transcending),
> which marks each major wave of self development.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 7

> His books The Evolving Self and In Over Our Heads show why a developmental approach
> is so important (and why Kegan is everybody's favorite son).
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 7

> ...from Sri Ramana Maharshi to Robert Kegan—during psychological development, the
> "I" of one stage becomes a "me" at the next.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 3
