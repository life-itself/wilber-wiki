# Jacques Derrida

## Wilber's View

Derrida gets more careful, sympathetic handling from Wilber than the average
"deconstructive postmodernist" — and then, in the same book, gets filed as an example of
[[postmodernism|postmodernism]]'s worst failure mode. On the sympathetic side, Wilber
treats *différance* as essentially compatible with his own holonic ontology: he reads
Derrida (via Jonathan Culler) as saying that meaning is *context-bound*, not that truth
doesn't exist — and since every context is itself a whole nested in a larger context
without end, Wilber recasts Derrida's "nothing is ever simply present" as just another
way of stating that everything is a holon, a whole/part with no final, self-sufficient
resting point. He even singles Derrida out, "uncharacteristically," for treating Hegel
with real respect, tracing Derrida's critique of self-contained "presence" back to the
same dialectical lineage (Hegel, then Bradley, then Nagarjuna) that Wilber's own
Emptiness-teaching draws on. But Wilber's verdict on where deconstruction actually lands
is blunt: next to that lineage of philosopher-mystics, "the deconstructionists are very
thin soup indeed" — because they stop at declaring everything self-contradictory and
unstable, and miss what Wilber takes to be the punch line those mystics were making: if
you don't want to rest in permanent self-contradiction, you must rest in infinity.
Instead, deconstruction's American form treats the study of nested truths as if it were
the study of nested *lies*, which is exactly why Wilber holds it responsible — alongside
Foucault, Nietzsche, and the rest of the postmodern lineage — for tipping vision-logic's
real insight about context into outright nihilism.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> [!quote]- "Derrida himself defines the notion very simply as 'the process of differentiation.'"
> And for all the commotion surrounding Derrida's notion of *différance* (to differ and
> defer)—some critics have used the notion to deconstruct anything they didn't happen to
> like—Derrida himself defines the notion very simply as "the process of
> differentiation." Before communication can begin, entities have to be differentiated
> in the first place, and in one sense these entities do not exist prior to the
> differentiating process per se.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 2, "The Pattern That Connects"

> [!quote]- "Derrida does not deny truth per se, but only insists that truth and meaning are context-bound"
> This is why Jonathan Culler, perhaps the foremost interpreter of Jacques Derrida's
> deconstruction, can point out that Derrida does *not* deny truth per se, but only
> insists that truth and meaning are *context*-*bound* (each context being a whole that
> is also part of another whole context, which itself . . .). "One could therefore,"
> says Culler, "identify deconstruction with the twin principles of the *contextual
> determination of meaning* and the *infinite extendability of context*."
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 2, "The Pattern That Connects"

> [!quote]- "Nothing, in either the elements or the system, is anywhere ever simply present or absent." (Derrida, quoted)
> The play of differences involves syntheses and referrals which prevent there from
> being at any moment or in any way a simple element which is *present* in and of itself
> and refers only to itself... This linkage means that each "element" is constituted
> with reference to the trace in it of the other elements of the system. Nothing, in
> either the elements or the system, is anywhere ever simply present or absent.
>
> — Derrida, quoted in *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 2, "The Pattern That Connects"

> [!quote]- "The deconstructionists are very thin soup indeed... they almost always miss the punch line"
> The deconstructionists have picked up certain of these lines of thought (mostly from
> Hegel, whom Derrida uncharacteristically treats with much respect), but after Bradley
> and Nagarjuna, the deconstructionists are very thin soup indeed, and, depressingly,
> they almost always miss the punch line: if you don't want to be a complete
> self-contradiction, then you must rest in infinity.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14, "The Unpacking of God"
