# Carol Gilligan

## Wilber's View

Wilber's IP treatment of Gilligan is sharper on one specific point than the parallel
material in *SES* (see [[kohlberg|Kohlberg]]'s page, which covers the SES-side
argument that Gilligan accepts Kohlberg's general hierarchical structure rather than
rejecting it): here he goes after what he calls a genuine cultural myth that grew up
around her work — the idea, drawn from a misreading of Gilligan, that women's moral
development *isn't* hierarchical or stage-based at all, only men's is. Wilber's
reading is the opposite: Gilligan's "selfish" → "care" → "universal care" sequence is,
in his framework, just another instance of the same egocentric-sociocentric-worldcentric
pattern that shows up across every developmental line he catalogs — a hierarchy in its
own right, not an alternative to hierarchy.

## In *Integral Psychology*

> Carol Gilligan...outlined a hierarchy of female moral development ("selfish" to
> "care" to "universal care," yet another version of egocentric to sociocentric to
> worldcentric), which had an enormous influence on the popular culture to precisely
> the degree it was widely misinterpreted (as implying that only males go through
> hierarchical stages; the idea that women do not go through hierarchical development
> became one of the most influential cultural myths of the last two decades).
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 7

> ...researchers from Gebser to Neumann to Gilligan to Loevinger also call specific
> stages by the actual name "integrated"—usually, their very highest level is given
> that name, not because the lower levels lack all integration, but because this
> highest level has the greatest amount of it.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 8 (footnote)
