# Susanne Cook-Greuter

## Wilber's View

Cook-Greuter supplies Wilber with the specific mechanism behind his "1-2-3 of
consciousness studies" framework (see
[The Big Three](../concepts/big-three.md)): her research reframes each cognitive stage
as a growing capacity to take perspectives, so that first-person (egocentric),
second-person (sociocentric), and third-person (worldcentric) perspective-taking
aren't just labels for stages but the actual developmental achievement each stage
represents. Her further claim — that postformal cognition keeps adding perspectives
beyond the third-person (fourth- and fifth-person) — is what gives Wilber's "1-2-3"
formula room to extend past formal operations into vision-logic and beyond, rather
than stopping at ordinary adult rationality. She's also presented as Loevinger's direct
successor: extending Loevinger's ego-development research into her own original model
rather than merely applying it.

## In *Integral Psychology*

> According to Susanne Cook-Greuter's research, preoperational thinking has only a
> first-person perspective (egocentric); concrete operational adds second-person
> perspectives (sociocentric); and formal operational goes further and adds
> third-person perspectives (which allow not only scientific precision but also
> impartial, postconventional, worldcentric judgments of fairness and care).
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 1

> ...these researchers all acknowledge the existence of yet higher, postformal stages
> of cognition—or a higher reason—which takes even more perspectives into account
> (fourth-and fifth-person perspectives, according to Cook-Greuter).
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 1

> Susanne Cook-Greuter has refined and extended Loevinger's research, and is forging
> her own original and important model of self development.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 7
