Text alternative

Wilber crosses two distinctions, interior/exterior (what a holon looks like from within, what it looks like from the outside) and individual/collective, and says every holon has all four of the resulting aspects at once. His worked example in Sex, Ecology, Spirituality ch. 4 is a single thought:

windowwhat the thought is from here (his words)how you find outvalidity claim
Upper Right · It · behavioral"a release of norepinephrine between the neural synapses in the frontal cortex, accompanied by high-amplitude beta waves"look: observe, measure, describe; "it can all be done in a monologue"truth ("matching map and territory")
Upper Left · I · intentional"even if you know what every single atom of my brain is doing, you will never know the actual details of my thought unless I tell you"ask, then interpret: "I must talk to you"truthfulness, sincerity ("whether the mapmaker can be trusted")
Lower Left · We · cultural"If I tell them to you and you don't speak the language, then you won't get the meaning"share a language; the meaning is held in a "shared cultural worldspace"justness, cultural fit, "mutual understanding"
Lower Right · Its · social"cultural meanings have correlates in objective social institutions and material social structures"survey the system: written records, school buildings, technology, legal codesfunctional fit ("What does it do?")

The point of the example is that the one thought "is not really a single thought as such, but rather a holon with four inseparable aspects", each correlated with the others and none reducible to another. Reducing the two left windows to the two right ones (a thought is "just" a brain state, a culture is "just" its observable behaviour) is what he calls subtle reductionism, and its result is flatland.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the two distinctions and the four quadrants with their names (Upper Left, Upper Right, Lower Left, Lower Right); the thought as the example holon and the order it is toured in (UR, UL, LL, LR); every quotation in the four windows and both passages below; the four aspect names (intentional, behavioral, cultural, social); the four validity claims (truthfulness, truth, justness, functional fit) and the map/mapmaker contrast; "I", "we", "it" and "its" as the pronouns of the quadrants; the list the Lower Right examples are taken from (written records, school buildings, types of technology, written legal codes).
  • Ours: the "window" framing and the title; the layout (four framed windows with the thought at the crossing, interior windows tinted); the sans "how you find out" line in each window (look / ask / share a language / survey the system), which paraphrases his "I just look", "I must talk to you", "mutual understanding" and "I look at a social system with empirical data and statistics"; the one-line validity glosses; the ghost of the thought flying to each window in the animation. All marked ◇ on the figure in one note.
  • Not shown on purpose: the developmental sequences Wilber lists inside each quadrant in his own figure 4-3 (atoms to brains, prehension to concepts, and so on): that is the published figure, and this piece shows one holon instead; the eight zones of Integral Spirituality (each quadrant seen from inside and outside), which the gallery sketch proposed as a second state and which would be a separate figure; subtle reductionism and flatland, which are on this page and on the concept page, not on the canvas.

For example, I have a thought; a thought occurs to me. That's the given holon, which we will use as an example. For this holon, in the Upper-Right quadrant, there is a change in brain physiology, a change that can be described in completely objective terms (it-language): there was a release of norepinephrine between the neural synapses in the frontal cortex, accompanied by high-amplitude beta waves . . . and so on.SES ch. 4 · line 1879 (subtitle; Upper Right window)
And even if you know what every single atom of my brain is doing, you will never know the actual details of my thought unless I tell you.SES ch. 4 · line 1881 (Upper Left window)
If I want to know what your brain looks like from within, what its actual lived interior is like (in other words, your mind), then I must talk to you. There is absolutely no other way.SES ch. 4 · line 1885 (passage, bottom right)
It can all be done in a monologue; it never needs a dialogue.SES ch. 4 · line 1889 (Upper Right "look" line; same paragraph: "I look at a social system with empirical data and statistics")
The validity criterion is one of truth, of matching map and territory accurately enough.SES ch. 4 · line 1903
It is not so much whether the map matches the territory, but whether the mapmaker can be trusted.SES ch. 4 · line 1905
If I tell them to you and you don't speak the language, then you won't get the meaning, even though all the physical words and stimuli freely bombard you. That is the Lower-Left quadrant, the shared cultural worldspace necessary for the communication of any meaning at allSES ch. 4 · line 1913 (Lower Left window)
…whether you and I can come to mutual understanding with each other. Not objective, not subjective, but intersubjective.SES ch. 4 · line 1919
That is, just as my thoughts have brain correlates, cultural meanings have correlates in objective social institutions and material social structures. Food production, transportation systems, written records, school buildings, geopolitical structures, behavioral actions of groups, written legal codes, architectural styles and the buildings themselves, types of technology…SES ch. 4 · line 1923 (Lower Right window and its examples)
Functional fit ("What does it do?") is, of course, the major validity claim recognized by systems theory.SES ch. 4 · line 1927
Now the point of this overall example is simply that my "single" thought, the original holon, is not really a single thought as such, but rather a holon with four inseparable aspects (intentional, behavioral, cultural, and social), each with its own validity claims (subjective truthfulness, objective truth, intersubjective justness, and interobjective functional fit).SES ch. 4 · line 1931 (passage, bottom left; the inner quotation marks are set as single quotes on the canvas)
