2000 · Integral Psychology · ch. 9, note 15

Wilber's own division of his work

I have, for convenience, divided my overall work into four general phases. Phase-1 was Romantic (a “recaptured-goodness” model), which posited a spectrum of consciousness ranging from subconscious to self-conscious to superconscious (or id to ego to God), with the higher stages viewed as a return to, and recapture of, original but lost potentials. Phase-2 was more specifically evolutionary or developmental (a “growth-to-goodness” model), with the spectrum of consciousness unfolding in developmental stages or levels. Phase-3 added developmental lines to those developmental levels—that is, numerous different developmental lines (such as cognitive, conative, affective, moral, psychological, spiritual, etc.) proceeding in a relatively independent manner through the basic levels of the overall spectrum of consciousness. Phase-4 added the idea of the four quadrants—the subjective (intentional), objective (behavioral), intersubjective (cultural), and interobjective (social) dimensions—of each of those levels and lines, with the result being—or at least attempting to be—a comprehensive or integral philosophy. The present book is, of course, a phase-4 work. For a discussion of these phases, see The Eye of Spirit and One Taste, Nov. 16 entry.Integral Psychology (2000), ch. 9, note 15 · full text line 2811

The gap, and the trilogy that stayed at volume 1

Sex, Ecology, Spirituality was the first theoretical book I had written in almost ten years, following the events described in Grace and Grit. The previous book, Transformations of Consciousness (with Jack Engler and Daniel P. Brown), was completed in 1984; I wrote Grace and Grit in 1991; and then I settled down to finally write a textbook of psychology that I had been planning on doing for several years.line 304
I am often asked when volume 2 of the trilogy will be published. My original plan was to release one volume a decade, which means volume 2 would be ready around the year 2005. But now I have no idea exactly when the other two volumes will be ready. Volume 2 is more or less fully written. Volume 3 exists in outline.line 440, “The Kosmos Trilogy”
This book is the first of three volumes (the series itself is simply called Kosmos, or The Kosmos Trilogy; brief summaries of the other two volumes are given throughout this book).line 474

How to read it

The axis is to scale, so distance is time: the shelf is crowded between 1995 and 2008 and nearly empty from 1987 to 1994 and from 2009 to 2013. Every spine is placed at its year of first publication as the catalog records it (spines in the same or adjacent years are nudged apart by a few units; the printed year is the date). Three weights of spine say how the wiki ranks a title, not how Wilber does: filled spines are the twelve books the wiki treats as core (the ★ selection in the catalog); outlined spines are the other books the phases page names as representative of a phase; thin ticks are everything else in the catalog, anthologies, edited collections and books outside the selection, labelled small. Dotted marks a retitled edition (Integral Buddhism, A Post-Truth World), an entry whose details are still marked draft (The Holographic Paradigm), and the second volume of the Kosmos trilogy, which was never published.

The five bands are periods, with soft edges because the phases page dates them approximately ("late 1970s", "around 1980–82", "1980s", "mid-1990s–2000", "early 2000s onward"); band V fades out to the right because it is open-ended. A book standing inside a band is not thereby a statement of that phase: Boomeritis is a novel, Grace and Grit a memoir, and the anthologies collect earlier writing. The phases page also notes that A Sociable God (1983) sits near the II/III transition and that commentators date the phases differently; the figure draws the wiki's dates and says so.

Text alternative

Phases, as the wiki dates them (periods, not boundaries): I, the early spectrum, late 1970s; II, development, around 1980–82; III, levels and lines, 1980s; IV, the four-quadrant synthesis, mid-1990s–2000; V, post-metaphysics and perspectives, early 2000s onward. Wilber's own sentences for phases 1–4 are in the passage above; phase V is not in his list.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the division of his work into four numbered phases and the one-sentence description of each (Integral Psychology, ch. 9, note 15, quoted verbatim in the key); that Integral Psychology is "a phase-4 work"; the gap sentence, "the first theoretical book I had written in almost ten years" (SES, revised-edition preface); the plan for Kosmos volume 2, "around the year 2005", and its status, "more or less fully written" but unpublished; the titles and years, which are bibliographic facts recorded in the catalog.
  • Ours: the shelf and the spines; the periods the bands cover and their soft edges (the dates in works/phases.md, which follow Wilber's retrospective and commentators); the phase names ("the early spectrum", "development", "levels and lines", "the four-quadrant synthesis", "post-metaphysics and perspectives"), which are the phases page's, not Wilber's; phase V itself, which is not in Wilber's 2000 list and follows later commentators (the page names Akalin's publisher guide and Helfrich); the three weights (core, named for a phase, the rest), which are the wiki's editorial selection; placing the unpublished volume at 2005.
  • Not shown on purpose: any line or arrow between books (recurrence and chronology do not establish inheritance; see the works-lineage review); Kosmos volume 3 ("exists in outline", no date); the Collected Works volumes, which the catalog says not to count as new books; the unpublished textbook System, Self, and Structure that Integral Psychology abridges; commentators' alternative phase dates (Helfrich dates by composition, Integral World by publication; the phases page records that they differ).
