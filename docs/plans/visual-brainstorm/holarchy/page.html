The tenet and the thought experiment

Destroy any type of holon, and you will destroy all of the holons above it and none of the holons below it. There is an enormous amount of confusion in the literature as to how to determine whether a given holon is “higher” or “lower” in the developmental sequence—not to mention the large numbers of critics who deny higher and lower altogether.Sex, Ecology, Spirituality (1995), ch. 2 · full text line 1112
But we can actually locate the level of a holon in any evolutionary or holistic sequence very simply, by asking ourselves, as a type of thought experiment, “What other types of holons would be destroyed if we destroyed this type of holon?”line 1114
For example, take the holistic sequence: subatomic particles, atoms, molecules, cells. Everybody agrees that that is indeed a holistic sequence—each member includes its predecessor(s) but not vice versa, and thus each successive member is indeed more encompassing (or more holistic).line 1116

Running it, and the rule it yields

But notice what that also unavoidably means: If we destroyed, for example, all of the molecules in the universe, we would also destroy all of the cells in the universe (all of the holons above molecules in the sequence), but atoms and subatomic particles would or could still exist (none of the lower holons would have to cease existence).line 1118
Molecules cannot exist without atoms; atoms can exist without molecules. There is nothing arbitrary in this arrangement; it is not the product of a particular “human value judgment.” It is true physically, and logically, and chronologically as well.line 1122
This allows us to easily determine what is lower, and what is higher, in any holistic sequence: destroy the particular holon, and everything else that is also destroyed is higher; those holons not destroyed are lower.line 1124

Why this is the test

"Hierarchy" sounds like ranking, and Wilber's critics read holarchy that way. Tenet 9 is his answer: higher and lower are not value judgements but a fact about what is made of what. A cell is made of molecules, so no molecules, no cells; molecules are not made of cells, so cells can go and molecules remain. The thought experiment turns that into a procedure anyone can run on any proposed sequence, and chapter 3 uses it to sort genuinely holistic sequences from regressive ones. It is a logical test on types, as he says, not a description of a physical event: "destroying every molecule" is a hypothesis to reason from, not something that could happen. concepts/holarchy.md and concepts/holons.md make the same case in prose.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the test itself and its wording (tenet 9, the thought-experiment question, the rule); the example sequence subatomic particles, atoms, molecules, cells, with organisms above cells from tenet 4 ("Organisms contain cells, but not vice versa; cells contain molecules, but not vice versa; molecules contain atoms, but not vice versa"); the choice of molecules as the holon to destroy; the result (cells go, atoms and subatomic particles "would or could still exist"); the "not vice versa" asymmetry; "atoms can exist without molecules".
  • Ours: drawing the sequence as a ladder and the test as pulling a rung out (Wilber speaks of levels in a sequence; "rung" is the wiki's picture, marked ◇ on the canvas); the before-and-after layout; the falling animation; the two-word glosses on the rungs ("contain cells", "needed molecules", "exist without molecules"), which are labels in the wiki's words condensing his sentences; the read-off bracket "gone with it, so they are higher / untouched, so they are lower", which restates his rule.
  • Not shown on purpose: the finer sequence he lists elsewhere (polymers between molecules and cells; plants, animals, societies above cells, line 1122); the "retreat" to the next lower level when a holon dissolves (line 1126); depth and span (tenets 7 and 8). One pull, one rule.
