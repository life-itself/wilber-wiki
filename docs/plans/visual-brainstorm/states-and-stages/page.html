One ladder in 2000; a ladder and a second axis in 2006. Press play to watch the four highest rungs leave the ladder and head the lattice; the last step is the image that travels on its own.

Integral Psychology (2000), chart 1: one ladder. The lower rungs are the familiar stages; above them sit psychic, subtle, causal and nondual. Those four highest rungs are the mystical states, drawn in 2000 as the highest structures. Integral Spirituality (2006): the same four words name states, and move across the top as a second axis. Every state can occur at every stage, and is interpreted according to the stage. Six stages by four states: the Wilber-Combs lattice, drawn here with its cells left open. This is the static image.

In words

In Integral Psychology (2000) the master chart's left-hand spine runs from archaic through magic, mythic, rational, pluralistic and integral up to psychic, subtle, causal and nondual: one column, with the mystical levels on top. In Integral Spirituality (2006) the same four words name states of consciousness, variations on waking, dreaming and dreamless sleep, and so available at every stage. Put the stages up the side and the states across the top and you have the Wilber-Combs lattice: a person at any stage can have a peak experience of any state, "but a person will interpret that state according to the stage they are at".

The honest caveat. What changed is the picture and the weight put on it, not the claim out of nowhere: Integral Psychology already says the major states "are available to human beings at virtually any stage of growth" and traces the grid idea to A Sociable God (1983). The 2000 chart nonetheless lists psychic, subtle, causal and nondual as the highest basic structures, and that is what the ladder shows.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: both pictures. The 2000 spine is his chart's left column; the 2006 lattice is built as he describes it, stages up the side, states across the top, with his names for both. "A person will interpret that state according to the stage they are at" is his.
  • Ours: drawing the spine as a literal ladder; the tint on the four words in both places and the one arrow between them, which is our picture of the shift; the rows of the lattice placed level with the ladder's lower rungs; the subtitle and headings. The six lower rung names are IS's Gebser-like list (archaic, magic, mythic, rational, pluralistic, integral), used on the ladder too so the two pictures share words; IP's chart 1 gives the worldviews as Archaic, Magical, Mythic (two rows), Formalism, Pluralistic-relativism, Holistic-integralism, so the pairing is approximate.
  • Left out on purpose: cell contents (Wilber gives examples of how states are read at particular stages; none have been quote-verified, so the cells are open); the one-word state glosses (waking, dreaming, formless) from the earlier build; the seventh stage Wilber counts in "7 stages × 4 states = 28" (the figure follows his six-name Gebser list). The first column is headed "gross", as IS lists the states; the same passage later writes "gross/psychic".
