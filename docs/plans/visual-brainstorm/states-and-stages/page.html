States off the ladder

design languagegallery

In Integral Psychology (2000) the transpersonal levels sit at the top of one developmental spine. In Integral Spirituality (2006) the same words name states that run across every stage. Press play: the four top rungs become column headers, the six lower rungs become rows, and the two pictures settle side by side.

2000 · Integral Psychology

One spine, and a caveat already in place

The master chart lists psychic, subtle, causal and nondual as the top of the structure spine (charts/chart-1-wilber-correlations.md). The same book already says states are available at any stage, and traces the grid idea back to 1983.

Nonetheless, although the major states of gross, subtle, causal, and nondual are available to human beings at virtually any stage of growth, the way in which those states or realms are experienced and interpreted depends to some degree on the stage of development of the person having the peak experience. This means, as I suggested in A Sociable God, that we can create a grid of the types of spiritual experiences that are generally available to individuals at different stages of growth.Integral Psychology (2000), ch. 1 · full text line 491
2006 · Integral Spirituality

The lattice becomes the organising picture

…put those sequentially developing structure-stages … running up the left side of the grid or lattice, and then put the major states across the top (gross, subtle, causal, nondual), you get a simple version of the W-C LatticeIntegral Spirituality (2006), ch. 4 · line 1207
…a person at any stage can have a peak experience of a gross, subtle, causal, or nondual state. But a person will interpret that state according to the stage they are at.Integral Spirituality (2006), ch. 4 · line 1214
Thus, gross/psychic, subtle, causal, and nondual were no longer stages stacked on top of the Western conventional stages, but were states (including altered states and peak experiences) that can and did occur alongside any of those stages.Integral Spirituality (2006), ch. 4 · line 1216

What changed, honestly

Less than the animation makes it look, and that is why both passages sit beside it. Integral Psychology already states that the four states are available at virtually any stage and that a stage × state grid can be drawn; Wilber dates the idea to A Sociable God (1983). What Integral Spirituality does is stop drawing the states as rungs: the vocabulary that had been doing double duty (psychic, subtle, causal as both structures and states) is split, the lattice becomes the organising figure, and the book builds its account of spirituality on it. The movement here is a change in the picture, and in what the picture is asked to carry, not the discovery of a new fact. concepts/from-ses-to-integral-spirituality.md makes the same case in prose.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: both pictures. The 2000 spine with psychic, subtle, causal, nondual at the top (chart 1); the 2006 lattice with stages up the side and states across the top, built exactly as he describes it; the stage names (Gebser-like list, IS ch. 4) and state names; the rule that a state is interpreted according to the stage.
  • Ours: presenting the two as one animation in which rungs become headers and rows (a framing of his "no longer stacked on top … alongside"); pairing IS stage names with IP chart-1 worldview names on the small labels (approximate: "mythic" spans two chart-1 rows); the one-word state glosses (waking, dreaming, formless sleep) from IS's "gross-waking, subtle-dreaming, and causal-formlessness"; the note marked ◇ on the figure.
  • Not shown on purpose: cell contents. Wilber gives examples of how particular states are read at particular stages; none have been quote-verified for this page, so cells carry only "stage × state" (hover a cell for the sentence that the label abbreviates).
