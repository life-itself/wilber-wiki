Taking states off the ladder

← back to the options gallery

In Integral Psychology (2000) the transpersonal levels sit at the top of one developmental spine. In Integral Spirituality (2006) the same words name states that run across every stage. Press play and watch the four top rungs leave the ladder and become a second axis: the Wilber-Combs lattice.

In the lattice, hover or tab to a cell.
2000 · Integral Psychology

One spine, and a caveat already in place

The book's master chart lists psychic, subtle, causal and nondual as the top of the structure spine (see charts/chart-1-wilber-correlations.md). But the same book already says states are available at any stage, and traces the grid idea back to 1983.

Nonetheless, although the major states of gross, subtle, causal, and nondual are available to human beings at virtually any stage of growth, the way in which those states or realms are experienced and interpreted depends to some degree on the stage of development of the person having the peak experience. This means, as I suggested in A Sociable God, that we can create a grid of the types of spiritual experiences that are generally available to individuals at different stages of growth.Integral Psychology (2000), ch. 1 (full text line 491)
2006 · Integral Spirituality

The lattice becomes the organising picture

…put those sequentially developing structure-stages … running up the left side of the grid or lattice, and then put the major states across the top (gross, subtle, causal, nondual), you get a simple version of the W-C LatticeIntegral Spirituality (2006), ch. 4 (full text line 1207)
…a person at any stage can have a peak experience of a gross, subtle, causal, or nondual state. But a person will interpret that state according to the stage they are at.Integral Spirituality (2006), ch. 4 (line 1214)
Thus, gross/psychic, subtle, causal, and nondual were no longer stages stacked on top of the Western conventional stages, but were states (including altered states and peak experiences) that can and did occur alongside any of those stages.Integral Spirituality (2006), ch. 4 (line 1216)

What changed, honestly

Less than the animation makes it look, and that is the point of showing both passages. Integral Psychology already states that the four states are available at virtually any stage and that a stage × state grid can be drawn; Wilber dates that idea to A Sociable God (1983). What Integral Spirituality does is stop drawing the states as rungs. The vocabulary that had been doing double duty (psychic, subtle, causal as both structures and states) is split, the lattice becomes the organising figure, and the book builds its account of spirituality on it. The movement in this animation is a change in the picture, and in what the picture is asked to carry, not the discovery of a new fact. concepts/from-ses-to-integral-spirituality.md makes the same case in prose.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: both pictures. The 2000 spine with psychic, subtle, causal, nondual at the top (chart 1); the 2006 lattice with stages up the side and states across the top (his own description, quoted above); the stage names (Gebser-like list in IS ch. 4) and state names; the claim that a state is interpreted according to the stage.
  • Ours: showing the two as one animation in which rungs become column headers; the pairing of IS stage names with IP chart-1 worldview names on the small labels (the two books use different vocabularies, and the pairing is approximate: "mythic" spans two chart-1 rows); the one-word state glosses (waking, dreaming, deep formless sleep) are taken from IS's statement that the states are "variations on the natural states of gross-waking, subtle-dreaming, and causal-formlessness"; "ever-present" for nondual is our gloss and should be checked.
  • Not shown on purpose: cell contents. Wilber gives examples of how particular states are interpreted at particular stages; none are drawn here because none have been quote-verified for this page. Cells show only "stage × state".
