The Pre/Trans Fallacy

design languagestates off the laddergallery

Three tiers of development and the two mistakes that skip the middle one. Press play to watch the two errors arrive; the last step is the image that travels on its own.

Development runs through three tiers: prerational, rational, transrational. The outer two share a tint because both are nonrational, and to an eye that has not sorted the sequence they look alike. Reductionism reads the transrational down as prerational. Wilber's example is Freud, for whom mystical experience is a regression to infantile states. Elevationism reads the prerational up as transrational. Wilber's example is Jung. Both arrows skip the rational tier: that is the fallacy. This is the static image.

In words

Wilber's account of development runs through three broad bands: prerational (infantile, magical, mythic: before reason), rational (the ego and formal reason) and transrational (mystical, contemplative, postformal: beyond reason, not beneath it). Because the first and third are both nonrational, an observer who has not sorted out the sequence sees only two kinds of thing, rational and nonrational, and the prerational and the transrational collapse into one. Two errors follow. Reductionism reads the transrational down as prerational (Freud). Elevationism reads the prerational up as transrational (Jung). Wilber's point is not that one side is right: both are "half right and half wrong".

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the three bands and their names; the claim that pre and trans look alike "to the untutored eye"; the two fallacies, their names and directions; Freud and Jung as the pair; "both half right and half wrong".
  • Ours: the stack as a picture, with the two nonrational tiers in one tint and the rational tier left open between them; the two arrows beside the stack, each skipping the middle tier; the one-line tier descriptions, summarised from concepts/pre-trans-fallacy.md; the subtitle's wording. Nothing on the canvas is set as a quotation.
  • Left out on purpose: Campbell and Eco-Romanticism (SES's extended cases); the Engler line "you have to be somebody before you can be nobody" (a footnote, and Engler's sentence); the earlier build's "untutored eye" bins, which drew the resemblance as a second diagram when the shared tint says it in one.
