Text alternative

Wilber's account of development runs through three broad bands: prerational (infantile, egocentric, magical and mythic: before reason), rational (the ego and formal reason) and transrational (mystical, contemplative, postformal: beyond reason, not beneath it). He summarises the sequence as pre-ego, ego, trans-ego. Because the first and third bands are both nonrational, an observer who hasn't sorted out the sequence sees only two categories, rational and nonrational, and the prerational and transrational collapse into one bin. Two errors follow. Reductionism reads the transrational down as prerational (his example: Freud). Elevationism reads the prerational up as transrational (his example: Jung). Wilber's point is not that one side is right: both are "half right and half wrong".

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the three bands and their names; "pre-ego to ego to trans-ego"; the claim that pre and trans look alike "to the untutored eye"; the two fallacies, their names and directions; Freud and Jung as the paradigm pair; "both half right and half wrong".
  • Ours: the two-bin "eye" as a picture of his resemblance sentence (marked ◇ on the figure); the fallacy arrows drawn along the spectrum's edges; the one-line band descriptions, summarised from concepts/pre-trans-fallacy.md; the collapse animation in step 1.
  • Not shown on purpose: Campbell and Eco-Romanticism (SES's extended cases); the Engler line ("you have to be somebody before you can be nobody"), which was on the earlier poster but is Engler's sentence from a footnote, so it now lives only here; any attribution of either error beyond the two people Wilber names in the quoted sentence.
