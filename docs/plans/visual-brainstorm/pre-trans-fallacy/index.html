Text alternative

Wilber's account of development runs through three broad bands: prerational (infantile, egocentric, magical and mythic: before reason), rational (the ego and formal reason) and transrational (mystical, contemplative, postformal: beyond reason, not beneath it). He summarises the sequence as pre-ego, ego, trans-ego. Because the first and third bands are both nonrational, an observer who hasn't sorted out the sequence sees only two categories, rational and nonrational, and the prerational and transrational collapse into one bin. Two errors follow. Reductionism reads the transrational down as prerational (his example: Freud, treating mystical union as regression). Elevationism reads the prerational up as transrational (his example: Jung). Wilber's point is not that one side is right: both are "half right and half wrong", since neurosis often is regression to prerational states, and mystical states do exist beyond, not beneath, reason. The healthy order, in Jack Engler's line that Wilber quotes, is that "you have to be somebody before you can be nobody".

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the three bands and their names; the phrase "pre-ego to ego to trans-ego"; the claim that pre and trans look alike "to the untutored eye"; the two fallacies and their names; Freud and Jung as the paradigm cases; "both half right and half wrong"; the Engler quotation.
  • Ours: drawing the untutored eye's view as two bins fed by dashed lines; placing the two fallacies as arrows along the edges of the spectrum; the short band descriptions (summarised from concepts/pre-trans-fallacy.md, which summarises SES ch. 6); colour and type.
  • Not shown on purpose: Joseph Campbell and Eco-Romanticism, Wilber's longer worked examples in SES; the "oceanic" Freud detail; any claim about which real people or movements commit which error beyond the two he names.
