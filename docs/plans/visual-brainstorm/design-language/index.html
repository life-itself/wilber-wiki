One language for drawing Wilber

A shared visual language for the series of Wilber visuals, shown on two real pieces rather than described. Serif is the book's voice, sans is the wiki's; four colour roles carry the meaning; every piece is one SVG source with a static image and an animation that settles on it. The settled spec is docs/figure-design.md (this folder's README has the tooling and the exploration notes); the twelve directions it applies to are in the options gallery. Seeds of Renaissance is the signature partner and the reference for restraint.

Palette: four roles and a neutral

Colour carries meaning, not decoration. A hue appears in a figure only when the thing it marks is in the figure. Light only (dark mode was dropped on 2026-10-26). Colours are tokens in visuals.css, so the palette can be swapped without touching the figures. Every text role passes 4.5:1 on paper and surface (contrast.py).

Decisions: the two fallacies in the pre/trans piece share one colour, because they are the same kind of thing; the first build coloured them differently and it read as a ranking. "State" shares the site's amber so the series feels native to wilber.wiki, so a figure never uses amber for both a state and a highlight. Rejected: Wilber's own altitude rainbow for levels (imports a late-Wilber convention into every figure) and monochrome (loses the state/structure distinction the states piece exists to make).

Type: the book's voice and the wiki's voice

Fraunces (serif) for titles, the names of Wilber's terms, and every verbatim quotation. Inter (sans) for labels, headers, locators, footers, controls and our notes. The reader learns without being told: serif is Wilber's words or his named things, sans is our apparatus.

Strokes and arrows

Four weights, three patterns, and a rule about arrows: a marker goes on a line only when the source gives the direction. Curved arrows are welcome wherever they read better than straight ones; a label sits beside the middle of its own arrow, on that arrow's side.

Label, verbatim quotation, marked interpretation

Three kinds of text, three looks, on the canvas and on the page. A quotation is always serif in curly quotes with a sans locator under it; the line number of the library/ text lives on the page, not the canvas. Anything that is our device or our judgement is sans italic muted with a ◇ in front, and the footer explains the mark once. Every canvas is signed bottom right with the Seeds of Renaissance mark and WILBER WIKI · SEEDS OF RENAISSANCE · wilber.wiki, embedded as a data URI so the SVG travels on its own.

Motion: an animation always settles on the static frame

The source SVG is authored in its settled state; earlier steps only subtract. Four appearance verbs (wv-fade, wv-draw, wv-move, wv-ghost) on elements with data-at="k". Fade 420 ms, move 900 ms, draw 700 ms, ease cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1). With prefers-reduced-motion the steps become cuts and Play jumps to the end; without JS the markup is already the settled frame. Try it:

Share crops

The series canvas is 1200 × 800. The Open Graph crop is its top 1200 × 630 (title and figure, no passages); the square is the full canvas letterboxed. Both are viewBox crops declared on the SVG root and rendered by export.py, so they never drift from the source.

Embedding in Flowershow

  • Copy visuals.css and wv-motion.js to assets/visuals/ and add them to config.json head exactly as the roadmap stylesheet and the annotator script are loaded today. Everything is scoped under .wv-root; the script returns at once when no [data-wv-steps] is on the page.
  • In a markdown page, paste the piece's figure block as raw HTML (the <div class="wv-root" data-wv-steps…> from its page.html, with source.svg inside).
  • For a header image or a share card use the exported SVG or PNG with an <img>; the standalone SVG carries its own tokens.
  • Edit source.svg and page.html, run python3 design-language/export.py, never edit the outputs.

Open questions for Rufus

  1. Serif for Wilber's words, sans for ours: keep as a rule for the whole series?
  2. Both fallacies in one colour (madder) rather than the earlier amber/rose pair: more principled, but is it less readable?
  3. The square is letterboxed rather than recomposed; a true square layout would mean a second arrangement per piece. Acceptable?
  4. The pre/trans canvas no longer carries the Engler line; it is on the page. Keep it off the image?
  5. Is the ladder → lattice animation an honest picture given that Integral Psychology already states the caveat? Both passages and the ◇ note are on the canvas; an alternative is a static diptych with no motion.
