Two ways to flatten the Kosmos

one thought, four windowsdesign languagegallery

The sequel to One thought, four windows. Same grid, same windows, and the point Wilber built the example for: there are two ways to flatten it. The gross reductionist empties every window into the Upper Right and then into atoms. The subtle reductionist, the systems "holist", keeps the whole web in view and empties only the interior windows into their exterior correlates, so the picture still looks whole. Press play to watch both collapses; step 5 is the poster.

Text alternative

Wilber distinguishes two reductionisms in Sex, Ecology, Spirituality ch. 4, both measured against the four quadrants (interior/exterior crossed with individual/collective; see the previous figure). Every interior has an exterior correlate (a thought has a brain state, a culture has institutions), which is what makes both reductions tempting and the second one hard to spot.

The figure draws this as three copies of the quadrant grid: the Kosmos with all four windows filled; gross reductionism with three windows emptied (dotted outlines) and arrows into an Upper Right that holds only atoms; subtle reductionism with the two interior windows emptied, arrows carrying I to It and We to Its, and the exterior half covered by one web of connected nodes, "the great interlocking web". The interior tint from the previous figure is present only in the first grid: in both reductions the within is gone. The result of subtle reductionism is what Wilber calls flatland.

What is Wilber's and what is ours

  • His: the two reductionisms and their names; gross reductionism's two moves (all quadrants to the Upper Right, then the Upper Right to atoms) and subtle reductionism's one move (the Left half to its correlates on the Right half, "I" and "we" to "its"); the quadrant grid and its pronouns, from the previous figure; "atoms" as what gross reductionism leaves and "the great interlocking web" as what subtle reductionism leaves; the examples behind the arrow labels ("thoughts are just brain states"; "Cultural meaning (LL) is reduced to social integration (LR)"); both verdicts; both lists of targets (the Epicureans, Holbach, La Mettrie; the systems theorists, structural/functionalists, "new paradigm" and "ecological/holistic" theories); the claim that subtle reductionism was the downside of the Enlightenment; every quotation on the canvas and the page.
  • Ours: the title and the three-grid layout (the Kosmos beside its two flattenings); the dotted empty windows as the mark of what a reduction has removed; the arrows (Wilber describes the direction of each reduction, "to the Upper Right", "the Left half to … the Right half", so the arrows follow his sentences but the drawing is ours); the web drawing over the exterior half, and the atom dots; the sans summary lines under each grid and the one-word glosses in the windows ("a meaning", "a brain state", "a shared language", "a social system"); the ghost flights and the order of the animation. Marked ◇ on the figure in one note.
  • Not shown on purpose: the Big Three → Big One version of the same argument (concepts/big-three.md; a possible separate figure); the Ego and Eco camps of ch. 12, both of which Wilber places inside flatland; Habermas's "colonization of the lifeworld" and Foucault's biopower, which he brings in as witnesses against systems theory (on the page's sources, not the canvas); the reduction of "truth" itself to functional fit. One distinction, two collapses.
  • A caution on the arrows. Wilber says the subtle reductionist collapses the Left half "to its correlates on the Right half" and that "cultural meaning (LL) is reduced to social integration (LR)"; the figure draws the two collapses as Upper Left → Upper Right and Lower Left → Lower Right. He also says, more loosely, that everything is reduced "to the Lower-Right quadrant of functional fit", which the single web over both exterior windows is meant to carry: the brain state and the social system become strands in one system.

Gross reductionism first reduces all quadrants to the Upper-Right quadrant, and second—this is the gross part—then reduces all the higher-order structures of the Upper-Right quadrant to atomic or subatomic particles. The result is purely materialistic, mechanistic (usually), atomistic (always). Historically, only a very few, highly gifted nuts have actually taken this path with any sort of brilliance, starting with the Epicureans and running to Holbach, La Mettrie, and the likeSES ch. 4 · line 1843 (the gross grid's two moves, "atoms", and its targets)
Gross reductionists, first of all, do not believe any interiors exist anywhereSES ch. 4 · line 1841 ("no interiors anywhere", the gross grid's summary line)
Opposed to these flatland atomists are the flatland holists. They do not reduce all holons to atoms. Rather, they reduce everything on the Left half of the diagram to a corresponding reality on the Right half. That is, they are the systems theorists and the structural/functionalists, in all their forms, from General System Theory to modern dynamic systems theory to many of the "new paradigm" and "ecological/holistic" theories.SES ch. 4 · line 1845 (the subtle grid's targets)
They claim to embrace the whole of reality, when in fact they have just devastated half of it.SES ch. 4 · line 1847
What these systems and "holistic" theorists don't seem to understand is that while they have indeed avoided gross reductionism—and for that are to be highly praised—they are nonetheless (and apparently unknown to them) the exemplars of subtle reductionism. They don't reduce everything to atoms; they reduce everything in the Left-Hand to a Right-Hand description in the "system." They reduce a four-quadrant holism (or Kosmic holism) to merely a Right-Hand holism, a flatland holism. And they are so understandably proud that they are holists, they overlook the flatland part.SES ch. 4 · line 1851 (passage, bottom left; the inner quotation marks are set as single quotes on the canvas)
Gross reductionism still rears its peculiar head every now and then … but by and large it has not been historically that influential, and certainly not as influential as many new-paradigm critics seem to think.SES ch. 4 · line 1853 (the gross grid's verdict)
The truly devastating contributor to modern flatland ontology, to an erasure of the Kosmos, has been the Right-Hand path of systems theory or structural/functionalism in its many forms. … Whereas all they have actually done is use a subtle reduction to overcome a gross one.SES ch. 4 · line 1855 (the subtle grid's verdict)
No, the downside of the Enlightenment was that it took a Kosmos of both Left and Right dimensions and reduced it to a cosmos that could be empirically (or monologically) described: it collapsed the Left half to its correlates on the Right half. Its great crime was not gross reductionism but subtle reductionism. The Great Holarchy of Being was collapsed into a "harmonious whole of interlocking orders," as John Locke put it, but orders that now had no within, no interiors, no qualitative distinctions, but instead could be approached through an objectifying and empiricist gaze (all being equal strands in the flatland web).SES ch. 4 · line 1863 (passage, bottom right; the subtle grid's sub-head)
In short, the Left was reduced to the Right, and thus interiors tended to get lost and flattened into mere exteriors … Interpretation, consciousness, and interior depth were converted (reduced) to exterior, objective, systems interaction—"I" and "we" were reduced to holistic "its"—the precise essence of subtle reductionism.SES ch. 4 · line 1865 (the arrow labels "I reduced to It", "We reduced to Its"; the title's "flatten")
…a flatland web that replaced interior depth with the great universal system of interlocking empirical surfaces, and thus reduced all interiors to exteriorly perceived strands in the great functional web. This "holistic" paradigm sounded like it covered "all" of reality (as against the atomists), but in fact it had just violently torn the Kosmos in half, tossed out depth and interiority, and settled aggressively on exteriors, surfaces, and the great interlocking webSES ch. 4 · line 1871 ("the great interlocking web", the web's label; the same phrase at line 1865)
Precisely because there are correlations between all four quadrants, this type of reductionism can be aggressively carried out. This is what makes subtle reductionism so hard to spot and so hard to redress.SES ch. 4 · line 1877 (the Kosmos grid's summary line)
…all holons possess these four quadrants, and each quadrant is intimately correlated with, dependent upon, but not reducible to, the others.SES ch. 4 · line 1931 (quotation under the Kosmos grid)
…I can always maintain that thoughts are just brain states, even though I will probably maintain (as most brain theorists do) that the brain states themselves are higher-order (or hierarchical) emergent patterns that cannot be reduced to their atomistic elements.SES ch. 4 · line 1933 (the "thoughts as brain states" arrow label; the next paragraph, line 1935: "This position is not yet gross reductionism. It is subtle reductionism.")
Cultural meaning (LL) is reduced to social integration (LR), yet another version of functional fit.SES ch. 4 · line 1999 (the "culture as social integration" arrow label)
As we will see in chapter 12, this subtle reductionism was the fundamental Enlightenment paradigm.SES ch. 4 · line 1951

This is subtle reductionism." (line 4869). Concept pages: concepts/flatland.md, concepts/four-quadrants.md, concepts/big-three.md.
