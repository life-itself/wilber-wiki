reader-facing pages first (paths below are proposals):

1. `works/reading-guide.md` — **What to read, and why**. A selective annotated

   question. Avoid reproducing the long descriptions on individual book pages.
2. `works/phases.md` — **The evolution of Wilber's thought**. Explain Wilber I–V,
   what changed, representative books, and what earlier claims were revised.
   These are periods of his thinking, not developmental levels assigned to readers.

Initially put thematic routes and a Kosmos trilogy sidebar within those pages.
Split out `works/themes.md` or `works/kosmos-trilogy.md` only when the material
warrants it. Link these guides from the chronological index without regrouping it.

Alternatives considered: one large guide is easier to maintain but conflates reading
advice with intellectual history; separate pages for every theme create unnecessary
fragmentation. Two pages give the two main questions enough room.

## The other useful organizing principle: contribution and overlap

Phase and subject alone do not answer whether a book is worth reading after another.
Classify its purpose: theoretical advance, accessible introduction, synthesis,
application, practice manual, memoir/journal, anthology, or edition of an existing
work. Allow more than one purpose. Keep editorial importance separate from ease of
entry: the current `core` list mixes these.

Each short annotation should cover:

- The question the book addresses and its distinctive contribution.
- Why read it / who it is for.
- Overlap: read instead of, after, or alongside which other book, and why.
- Its phase and subject, with qualifications where needed.

Distinguish bibliographic identity (a retitled edition), author-described abridgment,
and editorial judgment about conceptual overlap. Do not label a book redundant just
because its vocabulary is familiar. Avoid unsupported overlap percentages.

## Provisional phase map and reading selections

The five-phase account is a useful starting point, not an exact publication-date
classifier. [Integral World's overview](https://www.integralworld.net/phases.html)
describes the shifts from an early return-to-unity framework, through developmental
psychology and multiple developmental lines, to quadrants and post-metaphysics.
It dates the phases approximately 1977–79, 1980–82, 1983–87, 1995–2001, and 2001 onward.
Boundaries and individual assignments need author-text checks before publication.

| Phase | Research focus | Representative reading candidates |
|---|---|---|
| I | Early spectrum and unity model | The Spectrum of Consciousness; No Boundary as an accessible companion |
| II | Development rather than return; prepersonal versus transpersonal | The Atman Project; Up from Eden for the historical/cultural extension |
| III | Multiple lines of development and the self | A Sociable God; Transformations of Consciousness |
| IV | Four quadrants and the broad integral synthesis | Sex, Ecology, Spirituality; A Brief History of Everything as an accessible entry; Integral Psychology for psychological detail |
| V | Post-metaphysics, methods/perspectives, and the state–stage relationship | Integral Spirituality; The Religion of Tomorrow as a later extensive treatment |

Editorial starting hypothesis: for understanding the mature theory, prioritize
**Sex, Ecology, Spirituality → Integral Psychology → Integral Spirituality**.
Offer **A Brief History of Everything** as an easier entry to that route. This is
a proposed selection, not a claim that three books contain everything or that
Integral Psychology introduces a new phase.

For intellectual history, add Spectrum, Atman Project, and a phase-III selection.
For extended contemporary spirituality, assess Religion of Tomorrow. Treat Grace
and Grit and One Taste as another kind of reading, not theoretical prerequisites.

Thematic branches to research: psychology/development; religion and contemplative
practice; politics/culture; science and religion; lived experience. Candidate
comparisons include SES/Brief History, Theory of Everything/Integral Vision,
Integral Spirituality/Religion of Tomorrow, and Integral Life Practice/Finding
Radical Wholeness. These comparisons require contents and prefaces, not blurbs alone.

## What happened to the Kosmos trilogy?

SES is volume I. The local source's revised-edition discussion (around lines 394 and
440 in `library/1995-sex-ecology-spirituality-full-text.md`) describes an earlier
volume-II title, *Sex, God, and Gender*, says volume II is substantially written and
volume III outlined, and declines a definite publication date. These are historical
statements, not current status reports.

Later material is explicitly headed
[excerpts from volume 2](https://www.integralesforum.org/attachments/Excerpt_A-_Kosmos_II.pdf),
associated with *Kosmic Karma and Creativity*. Search retrieved this source but full
PDF retrieval failed in this session: inspect it before making detailed claims.
No separately published volumes II and III were established by this initial search.
Do not equate that with proof that their ideas never appeared elsewhere.

Research the changing announced titles, available excerpts, and documented reuse in
later books. Distinguish publication as a numbered volume from continuation of its
ideas. Do not simply call Integral Spirituality volume II or Religion of Tomorrow
volume III without explicit evidence. The eventual sidebar should answer both
“what was published?” and “where should I read the continuation?”
