# Excerpt critique: people pages

Critical review of the excerpt quotes on all 23 `people/*.md` pages, done after the
accordion/foldable-callout format was removed in favor of showing quotes directly (per
site owner feedback that the excerpt is the point). Since there's no more "expand for
more" fallback, each quote now has to earn its place on its own.

Method: each page's synthesis ("## Wilber's View") was read against its excerpts
("## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*"), then the surrounding passage for each quote was
read in `library/1995-sex-ecology-spirituality-full-text.md` (a page or two of context
each way, not just the cited lines), and assessed for fit, self-sufficiency, better
alternatives nearby, redundancy, coverage gaps, and length/trimming. Line numbers below
refer to that full-text file. This is a critique/report only — no page edits were made.

---

## Habermas

**Verdict: needs work** — the most serious finding of the whole review.

The single "quote" currently on the page is not a quote at all: it's a paraphrase
("Wilber's own description of Habermas, introducing him as a source whose conclusions
arrived independently at the same place as Wilber's") formatted as a blockquote with an
attribution line, as if verbatim. This is the only page in the set with a fabricated
excerpt and should be fixed first.

**Better alternatives found**, all verified in ch. 4 (~lines 1741–1985):

- The actual sentence being paraphrased:
  > "The other is the work of Jürgen Habermas, whom many (myself included) consider the world's foremost living philosopher and social theorist. The conclusions of these two sources are in strong agreement, even though they were arrived at independently and from very different angles." (ch. 4, line 1741)

- For the validity-claims → four-quadrants mapping (currently unbacked by any quote):
  > "Likewise, Habermas's three validity claims, for truth (objects), truthfulness or sincerity (subjects), and rightness or justice (intersubjectivity), refer respectively to the Right half, the Upper Left, and the Lower Left." (ch. 4, line 1975)

  or, in Habermas's own words as quoted by Wilber:
  > "With any speech act," he says, "the speaker takes up a relation to something in the objective world [it], something in a common social world [we], and something in his own subjective world [I]." (ch. 4, line 1981)

- For the "ally against flatland/instrumental reason" claim (also unbacked here, though present correctly on `foucault.md`):
  > "This is likewise what Habermas refers to as 'the totalitarian characteristics of an instrumental reason that *objectifies* everything around it, *itself included*.' ... In this respect, Habermas is in complete agreement with Foucault." (ch. 12, line 5169)

**Coverage gap:** all three claims in the synthesis (foremost-philosopher framing;
validity-claims→quadrants mapping; ally against flatland reason) currently have zero
real excerpt support. This page needs a near-total rebuild of its quote section.

## Piaget

The "formal operational transcends but includes" quote and the snail/relativity quote

synthesis frames as doing the real argumentative work — only gets the two above.

No stronger alternative found in the surrounding text (lines 2691–2867); the existing
selection is reasonable, just slightly imbalanced toward stage-color over the
egocentrism argument.

Adding this would give the page an actual Freud-in-his-own-words moment for its central
thesis.

## Foucault

**Verdict: needs work** — one clear gap, one clear upgrade, and an honesty-of-balance
problem.

The "Age of Man"/subject-not-object quotes and the Habermas-alliance quotes are
well-chosen (verified ch. 12 lines 4959–5347, ch. 13 lines 5563–5613).

**Gap #1:** the synthesis promises Wilber "tracks Foucault's own self-correction: the
early 'archaeological' Foucault... later recanted that approach as 'arrogant'" — but the
quote currently used for this (line 1208, an aside inside an unrelated
differentiation/integration discussion) never uses or supports "arrogant." The actual
on-point passage, a few hundred lines earlier in the same chapter, is unused:

> "(Foucault, in his reductionistic archaeology phase, actually proceeded in just this manner, bracketing *both* the truth *and* the meaning of linguistic statements, and just reported their systematic behavior, which caused quite an uproar. He later recanted and labeled the *exclusive* use of that approach 'arrogant.' But it is an arrogance that all such 'happy positivism' is prone to.)" (ch. 4, line 1797)

Should replace the current "abandoned archaeology" quote.

**Gap #2 (larger):** the synthesis's closing paragraph flags "the larger critique of
Foucault... postmodern antifoundationalism collapsing into its own kind of 'flatland'"
as present in the book "but less concentrated than the sympathetic material excerpted
here" — yet the page has zero quotes representing the critical side, making it read as
pure-ally despite the synthesis itself flagging real disagreement. Unused candidate:

> "Foucault, like all relativists, had exempted himself from the very criteria he aggressively applied to others. He was making an extensive series of truth claims that denied all truth claims (except his own privileged stance), and thus his position, as critics from Habermas to Taylor pointed out, was profoundly incoherent." (ch. 1, line 780)

**Redundancy:** the "pseudo-sciences... driven by power" quote and the "totalitarian
characteristics... complete agreement with Foucault" quote both illustrate the same
Habermas–Foucault alliance already made by a third quote. Trading one of these three for
the "arrogant" quote and the "exempted himself" quote above would bring the page's
balance in line with what its own synthesis claims.

Worth swapping in or adding — it's literally the source of the section heading the
synthesis refers to. Minor redundancy: quotes 1 and 2 both assert "Plotinus was
first/most complete," but each carries a distinct specific claim, so not worth cutting.

## Spinoza

**Verdict: needs work.**

Quotes are genuine and accurately transcribed, but there's a real fit problem, and a
substantially richer unused passage complicates the page's current framing.

**Fit problem:** the closing quote — "Spinoza, no doubt, summed it up best: 'The purpose
of Nature is to make men uniform, as children of a common mother'" — sits right after
the "Eco camps... recourse to Spinoza" quote, implying it illustrates Spinoza's role as
patron of Eco/nature-mysticism. But in the source (ch. 13, "THE ECO SLIDE INTO DIVINE
EGOISM," ~lines 5361–5387) this quote actually belongs to a *different* argument — about
Enlightenment-era rationalist uniformitarianism, which Wilber there attributes mainly to
the **Ego**-Enlightenment camp, not Eco/nature-pantheist thought. Correct quote, implied
argument the source doesn't make.

**Better alternative found (important):** a ch. 8 endnote gives Wilber's most careful
treatment of Spinoza's actual metaphysics, and it complicates the page's lead framing of
Spinoza as simple "pantheist":

> "Spinoza maintained that Spirit is radically other to this world, and yet totally and completely embraces this world as a logical premise embraces all its consequents... At no point whatsoever did Spinoza equate the sum total of finite nature with the whole of Spirit or God... This is why he himself differentiates carefully between Nature (as God or Spirit, *natura naturans*), which is infinitely transcendental, and nature (as this world, *natura naturata*), which is merely immanent." (ch. 8, endnote c27-c08r16, ~line 7485)

**Coverage gap:** given this endnote exists, the synthesis's simple "pantheism" framing
(current ch. 1 quote) understates Wilber's more careful transcendence-and-immanence
reading. Worth noting too: the synthesis's Latin tag *Deus sive Natura* does not
actually appear anywhere in the full text — accurate Spinoza scholarship, but not
sourced from *SES* itself.

## Kohlberg

**Verdict: adequate.**

Quote 1 ("Not everybody agrees with the details...") fits the synthesis's opening claim
but is truncated at its weakest point — the full sentence (line 476) continues into the
actual definitions of preconventional/conventional/postconventional, which would be far
more useful to a reader who doesn't already know the terms. As cut, it asserts agreement

> "Thus, we have seen moral development move from a *preconventional* orientation, which is strongly egocentric, geocentric, biocentric, narcissistic, bound to the body's separate feelings and nature's impulses (the first three fulcrums), to a *conventional* or sociocentric or ethnocentric orientation, bound to one's society, culture, tribe, or race, to a *postconventional* or worldcentric orientation, operating in the space of universal pluralism and global grasp (these stages have been elaborated by Piaget, Baldwin, Kohlberg, Gilligan, Habermas, Loevinger, Broughton, Selman, etc.)."

Suggest swapping this in for quote 1, or replacing quote 3 (the thinnest of the four)
with it. Quotes 3 and 4 are mildly redundant (both illustrate "postconventional") once
this addition is made.

## Loevinger

engages her on her own terms in *SES* beyond these pairings, so the fix here is
subtractive (cut the redundant quote), not additive.

## Derrida

**Verdict: strong**, with one high-value addition available — arguably the single
sharpest miss in the whole review.

All four quotes fit precisely and are self-sufficient (différance at line 1196, Culler
on context-boundedness at 888, "nothing is ever simply present" at 1200, "very thin
soup"/Hegel-Bradley-Nagarjuna lineage at 5881).

**Coverage gap:** the synthesis's strongest, most pointed line — "deconstruction's
American form treats the study of nested truths as if it were the study of nested lies,
which is exactly why Wilber holds it responsible... for tipping vision-logic's real
insight about context into outright nihilism" — is currently unbacked by any quote, even
though it's arguably the sharpest thing Wilber writes about Derrida in the whole book.
It exists verbatim in a footnote and is the near-literal source of the synthesis's
closing sentence:

> "The study of holarchy, in short, is the study of nested truths. Deconstruction in its American form simply takes the photographic negative of that and declares any approach (other than its own) to be the study of nested lies, which is precisely why that attitude lands it squarely in nihilism, whereas the study of nested truths leads not to nihilism but to Emptiness, the creative plenum of the Kosmos." (footnote to ch. 2, line 6415)

Short, punchy, fully self-sufficient — strongly recommend adding as a fifth quote. It
would give the page a quote that lands the "nihilism" verdict as directly as the "thin
soup" quote lands the lineage verdict.

Secondary/optional: line 890 has a punchier restatement of the Culler point already
covered by quote 2 ("Total context is unmasterable, both in principle and in practice.
*Meaning is context bound, but context is boundless*.") — not necessary as a swap, just
worth knowing it's there if the page ever wants to trim quote 2's longer formulation.

## Nietzsche

**Verdict: needs work** — weakest of the "core critics" cluster; one quote barely
evidences its claim, one clearly better alternative found.

"God is dead" (line 4559) fits exactly and is self-sufficient. The Taylor "genealogy"
quote (illustrating the "genealogy method... exposing hidden pragmatic contradictions"
claim) is reasonable but thin — it's really Taylor's argument, with Nietzsche cited only
as the source of a method-name; Nietzsche himself never speaks in it. The Tillich
"will" lineage quote (Schelling→Schopenhauer→Nietzsche→Bergson/Heidegger/Sartre, line
5745) fits strongly and is self-contained.

**Weak fit:** the "depths that range from Heidegger's clearing... to the mystical
openings found even in Nietzsche and Bataille and Derrida" quote is a long sentence
about postmodern "depths" broadly, where Nietzsche is one name in a list of four, not
argued about — it only loosely evidences the synthesis's specific claim that Nietzsche
"kept the door open to mysticism... despite themselves being creatures of rationality's
own overreach" (that exact framing isn't in the passage at all).

**Better alternative found:** the synthesis's opening genealogical claim — Nietzsche as
"starting point of the 'postmodern poststructuralist' lineage... through Foucault,
Derrida, and Lyotard" — is Wilber's own direct statement and exists as an unused,
sharper, more central match than the "depths" quote:

> "The 'postmodern poststructuralists'—usually associated with such names as Derrida, Foucault, Jean-François Lyotard, and stretching back to George Bataille and Nietzsche—have been the great foes of any sort of systematic theory or 'grand narrative'..." (ch. 2, "The Pattern That Connects," line 880)

or, shorter and equally strong:

> "From Nietzsche to Bataille to Foucault, from Heidegger to Derrida to Lyotard, the critics have continued their assault, with the postmodern poststructuralists being merely the most recent, although possibly the loudest, of the long line of antimodernists." (ch. 11, "Brave New World," line 4345)

Recommend swapping the "depths" quote for one of these.

## Weber

**Verdict: strong**, with one worthwhile addition.

All three quotes fit strongly and are self-sufficient: the rationality-typology quote
(line 2247), the "dignity... differentiation of the value spheres" quote (line 4809,
