Development runs from the **prerational** (infantile, magical, mythic) through the **rational** to the **transrational** (mystical, contemplative). The first and last are both *nonrational*, so they are easy to confuse. In Wilber's words:

> "The essence of the pre/trans fallacy is itself fairly simple: since both prerational states and transrational states are, in their own ways, nonrational, they appear similar or even identical to the untutored eye."
>
> Ken Wilber, *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995), ch. 6

The confusion runs both ways. **Reductionism** reads genuine mystical experience *down* as regression to infancy. **Elevationism** reads infantile or mythic states *up* as mystical union. "Freud was a reductionist, Jung an elevationist—the two sides of the pre/trans fallacy. And the point is that they are *both* half right and half wrong" (*SES*, ch. 6).

The two-bin "untutored eye" picture is this wiki's device for Wilber's sentence. More: [The Pre/Trans Fallacy](../concepts/pre-trans-fallacy.md).

In *Integral Psychology* (2000) the mystical states (psychic, subtle, causal, nondual) are drawn as the top four rungs of a single developmental ladder. In *Integral Spirituality* (2006) the same words name a second axis. They are **states** that can be experienced at any **stage**, and each experience is interpreted through the stage of the person having it:

> "…gross/psychic, subtle, causal, and nondual were no longer stages *stacked on top* of the Western conventional stages, but were states (including altered states and peak experiences) that can and did occur *alongside* any of those stages."
>
> Ken Wilber, *Integral Spirituality* (2006), ch. 4

What changed is mostly the picture and the weight put on it. *Integral Psychology* already says these states are "available to human beings at virtually any stage of growth" (ch. 1), and traces the grid idea back to *A Sociable God* (1983). More: [From SES to Integral Spirituality](../concepts/from-ses-to-integral-spirituality.md).

      <span data-cap="4"><strong>Its.</strong> Cultural meanings leave exterior correlates: records, buildings, technologies, institutions. The validity claim is functional fit.</span>
      <span data-cap="5">The settled frame: one holon with four inseparable aspects, each with its own validity claim, and Wilber's own sentences. This is the shareable image.</span>
    </figcaption>
  </figure>
</div>
<div class="wv-controls wv-actions" data-wv-actions></div>
</div>
<!-- /figure:four-quadrants -->

Wilber's four quadrants come from two distinctions: **interior** and **exterior**, and **individual** and **collective**. To show that they are four sides of one thing rather than four things, he takes a single thought round all four. From within, it is a meaning only I can tell you. From outside, it is a pattern of brain activity. Shared, it is a meaning that depends on a common language. Socially, it has correlates in institutions and structures. Each side is known in its own way and makes its own kind of validity claim:

> "…my 'single' thought, the original holon, is not really a single thought as such, but rather a holon with four inseparable aspects (intentional, behavioral, cultural, and social), each with its own validity claims…"
>
> Ken Wilber, *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995), ch. 4

The thought and every sentence in the windows are Wilber's. The "windows", the small drawings and the how-to-find-out lines are this wiki's. More: [The Four Quadrants](../concepts/four-quadrants.md).

How can you tell what is "higher" in a sequence without just asserting it? Wilber's answer in the twenty tenets is a thought experiment. Take a sequence where each member contains the one before: subatomic particles, atoms, molecules, cells, organisms. Then destroy one kind of holon and see what else goes.

> "Destroy any type of holon, and you will destroy all of the holons above it and none of the holons below it."
>
> Ken Wilber, *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995), ch. 2

Destroy every molecule and cells go too, and so do the organisms made of them. Atoms and particles carry on. "…destroy the particular holon, and everything else that is also destroyed is higher; those holons not destroyed are lower" (*SES*, ch. 2). The sequence, the test and the result are Wilber's. The ladder and the pulled rung are this wiki's picture. More: [Holarchy](../concepts/holarchy.md).

Every book in the [catalog](../works/catalog.md) at its year of first publication, to scale, under the phases of Wilber's thinking. Laid out this way, the shape of the work shows at a glance: a run of early books, then almost nothing between 1987 and 1995 except *Grace and Grit*, then a crowded shelf from *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* to *Integral Life Practice*. Wilber called SES "the first theoretical book I had written in almost ten years" (*SES*, preface to the revised edition).

He numbered the first four phases himself:

> "I have, for convenience, divided my overall work into four general phases."
>
> Ken Wilber, *Integral Psychology* (2000), ch. 9, note 15

Phase V is not in his list; the name is this wiki's, following later commentators. The bands are approximate periods, and no line between books claims that one led to another. Filled spines are the twelve core books; outlined ones are others named for a phase. More: [The evolution of Wilber's thought](../works/phases.md).

A sequel to [One thought, four windows](four-quadrants.md). Every interior has an exterior correlate, and that is what makes two kinds of reductionism tempting. **Gross reductionism** reduces everything to the Upper Right and then to atoms. **Subtle reductionism** keeps the whole interlocking system but collapses every interior into it, so thoughts become brain states and culture becomes social integration. Wilber says the second, not the first, is what built "flatland":

> "They don't reduce everything to atoms; they reduce everything in the Left-Hand to a Right-Hand description in the 'system.' They reduce a four-quadrant holism (or Kosmic holism) to merely a Right-Hand holism, a flatland holism."
>
> Ken Wilber, *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995), ch. 4

The two reductionisms, their verdicts and their targets are Wilber's. The three grids, the arrows and the web drawing are this wiki's. More: [Flatland](../concepts/flatland.md).
