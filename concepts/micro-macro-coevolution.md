# Micro/Macro Coevolution

## Wilber's View

Wilber's target here is a specific, recurring error he finds in popular systems-theory
and deep-ecology holarchies (he singles out Karl Popper's biospheric hierarchy as
typical): drawing individual holons and their social/collective environment as *the
same kind of thing*, stacked on the same ladder — atoms below cells, cells below
populations of cells, populations below tissues, and so on, as if a population of cells
and a tissue were just adjacent rungs of one scale. His diagnostic test for whether two
things are really at different levels is destruction: if destroying A also destroys B
but not vice versa, A is lower. Applied to "cells" and "populations of cells," neither
survives without the other — you can't have a population of cells with no cells, and
you can't have cells without an environment of other cells. They aren't two levels;
they're the **individual (micro) and social (macro) aspect of the same level**, "the
heads and tails of the same coin at every currency," not two different coins.

Drawing on Erich Jantsch, Wilber then makes the constructive claim: at every stage of
evolution, a new type of individual holon and a new type of correlative social holon
*coemerge* — heavy atoms and stars, molecules and planets, eukaryotic cells and
ecosystems, complex animals and families. This isn't just correlation; it's built into
his tenets (specifically tenet 11, "the micro is in relational exchange with the macro
at all levels of its depth"): no holon exists as an isolated individual, so wherever
depth increases, both a new individual pattern and a new environment for it must
appear together. One further wrinkle he flags, and it's a genuinely useful corrective
to naive "bigger is more evolved" pictures: as depth increases, *individual* holons get
bigger (molecules are bigger than atoms) while *social* holons get smaller (there are
fewer molecules than atoms, so collectives of them are smaller) — families are smaller
than ecosystems, which are smaller than planets, which are smaller than stars. The
weaker part of the argument is how much interpretive work Wilber does on Jantsch's own
diagrams ("I am in substantial agreement... with the exceptions I will mention") — the
coevolution pattern is clean for the physiosphere and biosphere, but by his own account
it "becomes somewhat garbled" once Jantsch reaches the noosphere, which is precisely
where Wilber most wants the pattern to hold.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> ...we notice immediately that there is a confusion and conflation of individual and
> social holons. That is, micro- and macroworlds are confused. The social holon is
> assumed to be of the same type and same nature as the compound individual holon, so
> that they can be arranged "above" or "below" each other.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 3, "Individual and Social"

> ...the individual and social are not two different coins, one being of a higher
> currency than the other, but rather the heads and tails of the *same* coin at *every*
> currency. They are two aspects of the same thing, not two fundamentally different
> things (or levels).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 3, "Individual and Social"

> The lower portion of each diagram represents microevolution—the evolution of
> individual holons. The upper portion represents macroevolution—the *correlative*
> level of social or environmental relationships (relational exchanges) in which the
> individual holons are inextricably embedded... When molecules emerge, planets emerge
> (i.e., they coemerge, coevolve). When eukaryotes emerge, ecosystems emerge; when
> complex animals emerge, families emerge.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 3, "Individual and Social"

> ...since evolution produces greater depth, less span, then the individual holons tend
> to get *bigger* (i.e., molecules are bigger than atoms, because they embrace and
> contain them), but social holons tend to get *smaller.* Because there are fewer
> holons at the greater depth... the collectives are smaller. Thus families are smaller
> than ecosystems, which are smaller than planets, which are smaller than stars.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 3, "Individual and Social"

> Notice in particular that both the micro and macro holarchies given by Jantsch (in
> both the physiosphere and the biosphere) constitute a genuine higher/lower
> relationship: destroy any lower (whether individual or social), and the levels above
> it are also destroyed, but not vice versa.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 3, "Individual and Social"

> Habermas begins with the observation that the *same structures of consciousness* (his
> phrase) can be found in the individual self (UL) and its cultural setting (LL), that
> is, in the micro and macro branch of the evolution of human consciousness.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 4, "A View from Within"
