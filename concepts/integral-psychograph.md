# The Integral Psychograph

## Wilber's View

The **integral psychograph** is Wilber's name for a specific diagnostic tool, distinct
from the reference charts elsewhere on this wiki: where a chart like
[Chart 1](../charts/chart-1-wilber-correlations.md) plots *models* — theorists' stage sequences
laid side by side — a psychograph plots one actual *individual*, level by developmental
line, to show how unevenly a real person's growth is distributed. The construction is
simple to state: put the basic-structures levels on the vertical axis, run several
developmental lines (Wilber's worked example uses five — cognitive, moral,
interpersonal, spiritual, affective) horizontally through them, and mark where a given
person's development currently sits on each line. Because the lines develop
relatively independently, the resulting profile is jagged rather than a flat plateau —
someone can be advanced cognitively while still early in moral or interpersonal
development, and the psychograph is the tool built specifically to make that
unevenness visible rather than averaging it away into one overall "level."

Wilber gives it real diagnostic weight, not just illustrative value: he treats an
individual's psychograph as the right unit of analysis for spotting "stick points" —
where a person's various lines have gotten stuck, dissociated, or arrested — and
describes it as the best available guide for therapeutic work, since a purely
level-based ("this person is at stage 5") or purely symptom-based diagnosis would miss
exactly the line-by-line unevenness the psychograph is designed to surface.

One qualification worth keeping alongside the tool itself: Wilber is explicit that the
vertical axis is not purely hierarchical top-to-bottom ranking — within any single
level, much of what happens is *heterarchical* (mutually interacting, non-ranked
elements), not further sub-stages, and he estimates this heterarchical material makes
up "at least half" of development. The psychograph's charted lines capture the
hierarchical migration between levels; they don't claim to capture everything
happening within one.

## In *Integral Psychology*

> All of this can be represented as in...what I call an "integral psychograph." The
> levels in the Great Nest are shown on the vertical axis, and through those levels
> run the various lines.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 2

> Of the two dozen or so lines, I give five as examples: cognitive, moral,
> interpersonal, spiritual, and affective.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 2

> The full Upper-Left quadrant includes the entire spectrum of consciousness as it
> appears in any individual, from bodily sensations to mental ideas to soul and
> spirit. The integral psychograph is a graph of this quadrant.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 5

> In other words, an integral psychograph of the individual is the best guide in
> this—or any other—therapeutic endeavor.
— *Integral Psychology*, ch. 8
