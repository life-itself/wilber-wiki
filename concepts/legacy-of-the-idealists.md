# The Legacy of the Idealists

## Wilber's View

Where [[hegel|the Hegel page]] tracks what Wilber does with one thinker, this page is
about Wilber's diagnosis of German Idealism as a *movement* — why, having gotten
further than anyone else in the modern West toward reintegrating Ascent and Descent
(see [[ascent-and-descent]]), it collapsed within decades of Hegel's death and left the
field to the [[ego-camp-and-eco-camp|Descenders]] uncontested. Wilber's summary of the
achievement is generous: Schelling and Hegel, in his reading, independently rediscover
the nondual insight that Spirit is already, eternally, fully present — not a future
goal but "already in full actuality accomplished" — and cast it as *vision-logic*,
Wilber's term for postformal, dialectical reason that holds opposites together instead
of splitting them. On his account the Idealists solved, at least in principle, the
central modern puzzle of how a self can be both radically autonomous (the Ego's demand)
and integrally whole with nature (the Eco's demand): both are true simultaneously *of
Spirit*, and to the degree one identifies with Spirit, both are given at once.

Wilber's diagnosis of the collapse has two parts, and the first is more interesting
than the second. First: the Idealists produced systems but no *practice* — no
injunctive, reproducible discipline (he contrasts this pointedly with Zen's zazen) by
which anyone else could verify the transpersonal claims for themselves, so the whole
edifice was "mistaken for metaphysics" and treated, fairly on Wilber's own terms, as
unfalsifiable speculation. This lands as a real methodological critique rather than
mere score-settling — it's the same standard Wilber holds his own "reconstructive
science" claims to elsewhere (see [[transpersonal-domains]]). Second, more
historically contingent: Idealism's account of nature had no room for the second law of
thermodynamics, and when 19th-century physics split biology's "winding up" from
physics' "winding down," the whole structure lost its empirical foothold. The two
failures don't actually support each other as well as Wilber's brisk pairing suggests —
a movement with genuine contemplative practice presumably could have survived a
scientific challenge to its cosmology in the same way a religion survives revisions to
its physics, so the "no yoga" critique is doing most of the real explanatory work, and
the thermodynamics story reads more like the occasion for the collapse than its deepest
cause.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> The Idealist movement was, in the West, the last great attempt to introduce true
> Ascent and, most important, to integrate it believably with true Descent—the Ego and
> the Eco both taken up, preserved and negated, honored and released, in
> all-encompassing Spirit. The true heirs of Plotinus.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14

> And although the great Idealist movement finally failed, its enduring contributions
> will, I believe, be part of any graceful unpacking of spiritual intuition for the
> modern and postmodern world.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14

> For the moment, I think we could fairly summarize the thrust of the Idealist movement
> as: *an intuition of the transpersonal domain expressed in vision*-*logic*. And there
> was both its great strength and its fatal weakness.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14

> The first was a failure to develop any truly *injunctive practices*—that is, any true
> paradigms, any reproducible exemplars. Put differently: no yoga, no contemplative
> practices, no meditative paradigms, no experimental methodology to reproduce in
> consciousness the transpersonal insights of its founders.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14

> This completely undercut any sort of idealist or spiritual view of evolution or
> manifestation in general. The first floor of the magnificent Idealist edifice
> crumbled, and the higher floors almost immediately followed suit (Idealism would
> survive in any viable form no more than a few decades after Hegel's death).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14

> The net effect of the utter collapse of this noble attempt to escape the Cave was
> that, once again, Ascent was looked upon very suspiciously... The collapse of
> Idealism left the Descenders virtually unchallenged as the holders and molders of
> modernity.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 14
