# The Psychic, Subtle, Causal, and Nondual

## Wilber's View

Having argued that transpersonal development is a genuine, cross-culturally
reconstructible sequence (see [[transpersonal-domains]]), Wilber lays out its four
stages, each illustrated with a single representative figure and a matching type of
mysticism. The **psychic** level (Emerson) is *nature mysticism*: the individual
witness begins to open onto an "Over-Soul" experienced as one with the whole of
manifestation — matter, life, and mind alike, not just nature narrowly but "nation and
nature" together, which is Wilber's way of blocking the reading of this as mere
biocentric immersion. The **subtle** level (Teresa of Ávila) is *deity mysticism*: the
soul unites with a personal or archetypal God-form beyond gross manifestation
altogether — Teresa's silkworm-to-butterfly metaphor for the death of the ego and birth
of the soul belongs here. The **causal** level (Meister Eckhart, Ramana Maharshi) is
*formless mysticism*: both the manifest world and the deity-form dissolve into
featureless, objectless awareness — the "Godhead" behind God, pure Witness, Atman =
Brahman. And the **nondual** (again Eckhart and Ramana) is not a further stage at all
but the recognition that the formless Ground and the entire world of Form were never
actually two — "Brahman is the world."

Wilber's case for treating this as a genuine developmental sequence rather than a set
of alternative belief systems rests on convergence: independently arising contemplative
maps (Mahamudra, Visuddhimagga, Yoga Sutras, the Christian, Sufi, and Kabbalist mystical
traditions) show, he argues, "an underlying common invariant sequence of stages" despite
enormous cultural difference in surface content. That cross-tradition convergence is
his strongest card, and it's a real one — it does more work than pure philosophical
argument could. What's more contestable is the confidence with which four named
representatives are made to stand for four crisply bounded stages; Wilber himself
acknowledges each stage has multiple substages, and reducing whole traditions to a
single spokesperson (Eckhart *and* Ramana are made to illustrate two different stages
each) is a simplification in service of narrative clarity that a specialist in any one
tradition would likely push back on.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> The evidence, though still preliminary, strongly suggests that, at a minimum, *there
> are four general stages of transpersonal development*, each with at least two
> substages... These four stages I call the *psychic*, the *subtle*, the *causal*, and
> the *nondual*.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> The word *transpersonal* is somewhat awkward and confuses many people. But the point
> is simply, as Emerson put it, "*The soul knows no persons*."... "Impersonal," however,
> is not quite right, because it tends to imply a complete negation of the personal,
> whereas in higher development the personal is negated and preserved, or transcended
> and included: hence, "transpersonal."
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 8, "The Depths of the Divine"

> At the subtle level, this process of "interiorization" or "within-and-beyond"
> intensifies—a new transcendence with a new depth, a new embrace, a higher
> consciousness, a wider identity—and the soul and God enter an even deeper interior
> marriage, which discloses at its summit a divine union of Soul and Spirit, a union
> *prior* to any of its manifestations as matter or life or mind... Nature-nation
> mysticism gives way to Deity mysticism.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 8, "The Depths of the Divine"

> In the subtle level, the Soul and God unite; in the causal level, the Soul and God are
> both transcended in the prior identity of Godhead, or pure formless awareness, pure
> consciousness as such, the pure Self as pure Spirit (Atman = Brahman). No longer the
> "Supreme Union" of God and Soul, but the "Supreme Identity" of Godhead.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 8, "The Depths of the Divine"

> Going *within* and *beyond* even this pure Source and pure Spirit—which is totally
> formless, boundless, unmanifest—the Self/Spirit awakens to an identity with, and as,
> *all* Form... This is not a particular stage among other stages—not their Goal, not
> their Source, not their Summit—but rather the Ground or Suchness or Isness of *all*
> stages, at all times, in all dimensions... And that is the *Nondual*.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 8, "The Depths of the Divine"

> The Godhead *completely transcends* all worlds and thus *completely includes* all
> worlds. It is the final within, leading to a final beyond—a beyond that, confined to
> absolutely *nothing*, embraces absolutely *everything*.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 8, "The Depths of the Divine"
