# Right-Hand and Left-Hand Paths

## Wilber's View

Wilber groups the [[four-quadrants|four quadrants]] into two "paths" defined by
methodology rather than by individual/collective scale. The **Right-Hand path** covers
both exterior quadrants (Upper-Right and Lower-Right) — anything that can be described
in "it"-language: seen, measured, registered by the senses or their instrumental
extensions, studied empirically, behaviorally, monologically. Its objects are, in
themselves, value-neutral surfaces — "nothing is better or worse, good or bad... The
surface forms simply are, and you simply observe and describe them." The **Left-Hand
path** covers both interior quadrants (Upper-Left and Lower-Left) — anything that has to
be interpreted rather than merely observed: meaning, intention, culture, value. Where
Right-Hand knowledge is validated by objective truth (does the map match the territory),
Left-Hand knowledge is validated by *sincerity* in the individual case (can the speaker
be trusted, are they self-deceived) and by *mutual understanding* in the collective case
(can we, through dialogue, come to shared meaning) — validity claims that empirical
observation alone can't settle.

The distinction is doing real argumentative work for Wilber, not just organizing a
diagram: he uses it to define [[flatland|subtle reductionism]] precisely as collapsing
Left-Hand phenomena into Right-Hand descriptions — treating "mind" as fully captured by
"brain," treating culture as fully captured by observable social behavior — while still
sounding holistic because the *correlations* between the two really do exist (every
point on the Right has a correlate on the Left). His example: a thought correlates with
a measurable change in brain physiology (Right-Hand, Upper-Right), but the thought's
actual meaning is something only the thinker can tell you, and even complete knowledge
of the neurochemistry will never disclose it (Left-Hand, Upper-Left) — correlation
without reduction.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> I will often refer to the study of both the Upper-Right and Lower-Right quadrants as
> the Right-Hand path: the path of that which can be seen with the eye of flesh or its
> extensions. In short, the path of "it"-language (objectivist, monological, observable,
> empirical, behavioral variables).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 4, "A View from Within"

> For example, I have a thought; a thought occurs to me. That's the given holon, which we
> will use as an example. For this holon, in the Upper-Right quadrant, there is a change in
> brain physiology, a change that can be described in completely objective terms
> (it-language)... But that is not how I experienced the thought, and I will never actually
> *experience* my thought in those terms. Instead, the thought had an interesting and
> important meaning to me, which I may or may not share with you. And even if you know what
> every single atom of my brain is doing, you will never know the actual details of my
> thought *unless I tell you*.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 4, "A View from Within"

> In the Upper-Left quadrant, the validity criterion is not so much truth as
> truthfulness, or sincerity. The question here is not "Is it raining outside?" The
> question here is: when I tell you it is raining outside, am I telling you the truth or
> am I lying?... It is not so much whether the map matches the territory, but whether
> the mapmaker can be trusted.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 4, "A View from Within"

> The criterion for validity in the Lower-Left quadrant is not just the truth of my
> statement, nor the truthfulness with which I put it, but whether you and I can come to
> mutual understanding with each other. Not objective, not subjective, but
> intersubjective.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 4, "A View from Within"
