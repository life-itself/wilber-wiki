# Validity Claims of Mysticism

## Wilber's View

Wilber's case that mystical claims can be checked, not just believed or dismissed,
rests on a three-strand model he says underlies *any* valid knowledge quest, empirical
or otherwise: **injunction** ("if you want to know this, do this" — get a microscope,
learn arithmetic, learn to meditate), **illumination** or direct apprehension (the data
the injunction discloses once performed), and **communal confirmation** (checking your
result against others who've completed the same injunction). His illustration is
deliberately mundane — is it raining? go to the window, look, and have someone else
check — to establish that this three-strand structure isn't something special he's
inventing for mysticism; it's how *any* domain, from meteorology to *Hamlet* to
arithmetic, actually generates knowledge people trust. He then leans on Thomas Kuhn to
make a specific, pointed argument: Kuhn's "paradigm" was originally a claim about
*injunctions* — shared exemplary practices that disclose data — not, as Wilber says it
was popularly (mis)read in both the humanities and the New Age movement, permission to
treat all knowledge as arbitrary social convention with no real referent. Read
correctly, Kuhn is Wilber's ally, not a relativist: "later scientific theories are
better than earlier ones," in Kuhn's own words, because paradigms are injunctions that
progressively disclose more data, not stories a community merely prefers.

Against that background, Wilber's argument is that the contemplative traditions
already satisfy all three strands — they come "first and foremost with a set of
injunctions in hand" (zazen, vipassana, contemplative prayer), these disclose direct
apprehensions (*satori*, *unio mystica*), and a community of the similarly trained
(the sangha) checks and can reject bad data — so contemplative knowledge is
structurally continuous with any other reconstructive discipline, not a separate,
softer category called "belief." This is a genuinely strong move against the crude
"mysticism is just private feeling" dismissal. What it doesn't fully resolve, though,
is the harder version of the objection: empirical communities converge on shared,
publicly checkable data because the injunctions (a microscope, a controlled
experiment) are largely observer-independent, while contemplative "communal
confirmation" happens within a single tradition's own sangha, applying that
tradition's own criteria for what counts as a valid or a "rebuffed" result — which
doesn't by itself explain how one would adjudicate *between* traditions whose trained
communities confirm different data. Wilber's answer to that harder problem is the deep
structure/surface structure move made in the very next section (see
[[reconstruction-of-the-contemplative-path]]), not this one.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> If I want to know whether it is raining or not, I go to the window and look out, and
> sure enough, rain. But perhaps I am mistaken, or perhaps my eyesight is poor. Would
> you check? You go to the window and yes, rain.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> The first is *injunction*, which is always of the form, "If you want to know this, do
> this."... The various *injunctions*... lead to or disclose or open up the possibility
> of an illumination, an apprehension, an intuition, or a direct experiencing of the
> domain addressed by the injunction.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> But you could be mistaken, and thus you check your results, your data, with others
> who have completed the first two strands, with others who have performed the
> injunctions and obtained the data. In this community of peers, you compare and
> confirm—or reject—your original data. And this is the third strand, *communal
> confirmation* (or refutation).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> All of this ignored Kuhn's repeated insistence that "later scientific theories are
> better than earlier ones for solving puzzles in the quite often different
> environments to which they are applied. This is not a relativist's position, and it
> displays the sense in which I am a convinced believer in scientific progress."
>
> — Kuhn, quoted in *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human
> Nature"

> These injunctions (*zazen*, *shikan*-*taza*, *vipassana*, contemplative
> introspection, *satsang*, *darshan*—all of which we will discuss)—these are not
> things to think, they are things to do.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> These data are rigorously checked (strand three) in the community of those who have
> also completed the first two strands (injunction and illumination). Bad data are
> *rebuffed* by the community (the sangha) of those whose cognitive eyes are adequate to
> the addressed domain.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> Accordingly, contemplative knowledge is, or can be, genuine knowledge, because it
> follows all three strands of valid knowledge accumulation.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"
