# Two Arrows of Time

## Wilber's View

Wilber opens *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* with a historical diagnosis of why the
[[great-chain-of-being|Great Chain of Being]] fell apart, and the mechanism he gives is
a genuine physics/biology conflict, not just a cultural or religious one. Classical
mechanics (Newton, Kepler) is time-symmetric — its laws don't care which way the clock
runs. But thermodynamics discovered a real "arrow of time": entropy only increases, a
dispersed ink drop never regathers, and the Second Law says the universe is a clockwork
*running down*. Then Darwinian biology discovered the opposite arrow: evolution is
irreversible in the other direction, moving from simpler to more complex, "winding up"
rather than down. Two sciences, both genuinely empirical, pointing in flatly opposite
temporal directions — and, Wilber argues, this (not the older mind/body split) is the
specific historical fracture that split matter from life from mind into the "two
cultures" of modern thought, since neither classical Cartesian dualism nor
reductionism nor epiphenomenalism could paper over a *contradiction in the actual
results*, not just a difference of perspective.

His resolution is that 20th-century complexity science (Prigogine's dissipative
structures, self-organization, "order out of chaos") closes the gap: matter itself,
under far-from-equilibrium conditions, spontaneously organizes into higher-order
structures — so the physiosphere's arrow and the biosphere's arrow turn out to point
the same direction after all, once you look at the right part of physics. This is a
clean, well-supported historical narrative and a genuinely important scientific point
(entropy and self-organization *are* both real, and both matter to any complete
picture). What's worth flagging as more Wilber's own framing than settled history: the
tidy sequencing — Great Chain intact, then shattered specifically by two-arrows
incompatibility, then reunified by complexity science — does a lot of narrative work to
set up his larger argument that traditional holism was basically right all along and
modern science is now "catching up" to it. The two-arrows problem is real; whether it's
*the* hinge on which the entire Western worldview turned is a much stronger claim, and
one he asserts more than demonstrates.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> But in thermodynamic processes, "time's arrow" is absolutely central. If you put a
> drop of ink in a glass of water, in a day or so the ink will have evenly dispersed
> throughout the water. But you will *never* see the reverse process happen—you will
> never see the dispersed ink gather itself together into a small drop.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 1, "The Web of Life"

> And the infamous Second Law of Thermodynamics added a dismal conclusion: the
> direction of time's arrow is downward. Physical processes, like the inkdrop, always go
> from *more ordered* (the inkdrop) to *less ordered* (dispersed throughout the water).
> The universe may be a giant clockwork, but the clock is winding down . . . and will
> eventually run out.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 1, "The Web of Life"

> And in the biosphere Darwin (and many others) noticed that there is also a crucial
> time's arrow. Evolution is irreversible. We may see amoebas eventually evolve into
> apes, but we never see apes turn into amoebas... But obviously, the direction of
> *this* time's arrow was diametrically opposed to time's arrow in the (known)
> physiosphere: the former is winding up, the latter is winding down.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 1, "The Web of Life"

> ...both physics and biology were *supposed* to be part of the new natural sciences,
> relying on empirical observation, measurement, theory formation, and rigorous testing
> ...But although the *methods* of physics and biology were similar, their *results*
> were fundamentally incompatible, saddled, as Laszlo put it, with "the persistent
> contradiction between a mechanistic world slated to run down and an organic world
> seeming to wind up."
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 1, "The Web of Life"

> As several researchers have noted, "not until the puzzle of the two and opposing
> arrows of time was resolved in the late twentieth century was there a sound basis for
> bridging the gap between matter and mind, the natural world and the human world, and
> thus between the 'two cultures' of modern Western civilization."
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 1, "The Web of Life"

> ...under certain circumstances matter will "wind itself up" into states of higher
> order, as when the water running down a drain suddenly ceases to be chaotic and forms
> a perfect funnel or whirlpool... The two arrows have joined forces.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 1, "The Web of Life"
