# The Transpersonal Domains

## Wilber's View

Having spent several chapters carefully tracing the archaic, magic, and mythic
structures that "religion" ordinarily gets identified with, Wilber's move here is to
insist that genuine contemplative development is precisely *not* that — it's a further
unfolding, beyond the mature rational-ego, into what he calls the transpersonal
("personal *plus*," not "personal minus," to head off the common New Age
misunderstanding that transcending ego means regressing behind it — see
[[pre-trans-fallacy]]). His case for taking transpersonal claims seriously as knowledge
rather than mere private feeling is structural: he argues *any* legitimate domain of
knowledge, from mathematics to literary interpretation to physical science, rests on
three components — an **injunction** ("if you want to know this, do this"), an
**illumination** or direct apprehension that the injunction discloses, and **communal
confirmation** by a community of others who have performed the same injunction. Since
contemplative traditions come "first and foremost with a set of injunctions in hand"
(zazen, vipassana, contemplative prayer) that reliably disclose data checked by a
community of the similarly trained, Wilber argues they satisfy the same three-strand
test any other reconstructive science does — they just aren't *empirical* science,
because their data lie on the interior, Left-Hand side of the [[four-quadrants]] rather
than the exterior Right-Hand side that empirical method is built to access.

He then works through the standard objections and, in each case, tries to show they
prove too much: if "private and unverifiable" disqualifies mystical experience, it
disqualifies mathematics too (nobody has ever seen the square root of negative one "out
there"); if "can't be put into words" disqualifies it, that disqualifies sex, sunsets,
and Bach as well. This is Wilber at his most rhetorically confident, and the parallel
to mathematics is genuinely clever — but it also glosses over an obvious disanalogy
critics would press: mathematical communities can produce formal proofs that settle
disputes in ways no comparable mechanism exists for adjudicating between rival
contemplative traditions that report *different* metaphysical conclusions. Wilber's
own answer — that surface disagreements mask a convergent deep structure, evidenced by
the psychic/subtle/causal/nondual sequence he goes on to reconstruct — is where the
real weight of the argument ends up resting; see [[psychic-subtle-causal-nondual|The
Psychic, Subtle, Causal, and Nondual]] for that payoff.

## In *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*

> ...we have sketched the evolution of the exterior of individual holons up to the
> complex triune-brained organisms... But then something happened that transformed
> hominids... into true humans... we are here beginning to pass out of the noosphere and
> into the theosphere, into the transpersonal domains, the domains not just of the
> self-conscious but of the superconscious.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> Their teachings, and their contemplative endeavors, were (and are) transrational
> through and through. That is, although all of the contemplative traditions aim at
> going within and beyond reason, they all *start* with reason, start with the notion
> that truth is to be established by *evidence*, that truth is the result of
> *experimental* methods, that truth is to be *tested* in the laboratory of personal
> *experience*.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> The most typical objection is that these mystical states are private and interior and
> cannot be publicly validated; they are "merely subjective." This is simply not true;
> or rather, if it is true, then it applies to any and all nonempirical endeavors, from
> mathematics to literature to linguistics to psychoanalysis to historical
> interpretation.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature" (section
> "Objections to the Transpersonal")

> The various *injunctions*, in other words, lead to or disclose or open up the
> possibility of an illumination, an apprehension, an intuition, or a direct experiencing
> of the domain addressed by the injunction... But you could be mistaken, and thus you
> check your results, your data, with others who have completed the first two
> strands... And this is the third strand, *communal confirmation* (or refutation).
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> The contemplative traditions, on the other hand, have always come first and foremost
> with a set of injunctions in hand. They are, above all else, a set of *practices*,
> practices that require years to master.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"

> ...the deep structures of worldspaces (archaic, magic, mythic, rational, and
> transpersonal) show cross-cultural and largely invariant features at a deep level of
> abstraction, whereas the surface structures... are naturally and appropriately quite
> different from culture to culture.
>
> — *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality*, ch. 7, "The Farther Reaches of Human Nature"
